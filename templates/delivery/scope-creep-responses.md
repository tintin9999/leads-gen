# Scope-Creep Responses — what to say in the moment

> **When to use:** When a client asks for "just one more thing" on a call, in chat, or in review comments. Scope creep usually starts with you agreeing on the spot. These scripts let you respond warmly without committing on the spot.
>
> **How to use:** The pattern is always **Acknowledge → Classify → Offer options → Log**. Then send the [CR email](change-request-email.md) within 24 hours. Keep a **goodwill budget**: up to ~1 hour per week of genuinely tiny tweaks can be absorbed, but log every one as a ₹0 CR.
>
> **Never:** say "sure, no problem" to anything you haven't sized, argue about whether something was "obviously included" (check the SOW), or work on unapproved changes over a weekend.

---

## The universal response
> "Love that idea. Let me check it against our scope and come back by tomorrow with options: add it now, swap it for something, or park it for phase 2. That way the launch date stays safe."

## Situation scripts

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Can you just quickly add…?" | "Happy to. I'll size it first so I don't promise something that slips the launch. You'll have options by tomorrow." |
| "This should only take 5 minutes." | "It might. If it's under 30 minutes I'll fold it in. If it touches {{checkout / sync / auth}} it needs proper testing, so I'll come back with a quick estimate." |
| "We assumed X was included." | "Let me check the SOW together. Section 2 lists A, B, C, and X is under 'out of scope'. I see why it feels natural to include, though. Want me to quote it, or swap it for something lower priority?" |
| "Our CEO saw it and wants changes." (after acceptance) | "Great that it's getting attention. Since this milestone was accepted on {{date}}, I'll treat the CEO's changes as a change request with options. Can I get 15 minutes with them to hear it first-hand?" |
| "Another round of revisions?" | "We've used the 2 included rounds. A third round is ₹{{amount}}, or we can lock the current version and collect any changes for a single round later." |
| "Can you also look at our other website / app?" | "Definitely, that's a separate piece of work. I'll send a short proposal so this project's timeline isn't affected." |
| "Urgent! Need this by Monday." (Friday evening) | "I can do an emergency turnaround at the out-of-hours rate (1.5×), or first thing Monday at the normal rate. Which works better?" |
| "Can you hop on a quick call?" (daily, unplanned) | "Sure. To keep the build moving, can we batch questions into our Tuesday call, or I can answer in writing today? If it's blocking you, let's do 15 minutes now." |
| "Let's change the design direction." (mid-build) | "We can. It resets {{components}}. Here's the cost and timeline impact. Alternatively, we finish v1 as designed and do the new direction as phase 2." |
| "Can you hand over the code now?" (final payment pending) | "Of course. The repo transfer and production deploy happen as soon as the final invoice clears. Here's the staging link to review everything in the meantime." |
| "Our budget is tight. Can you do it for less?" | "Let's keep the price and reduce scope instead. If we drop {{X}} and {{Y}}, I can do it for ₹{{lower}}. That keeps quality where it should be." |
| "Another dev said this would take 2 days." | "They may know a shortcut, and I'm happy to look at their approach. My estimate includes tests and edge cases ({{e.g. COD, failed refunds}}), which is where the time goes on payment flows." |

## Warning signs to escalate (bring it up in the weekly update)
- Three or more unsized requests in one week.
- New stakeholders appearing after kickoff.
- Feedback that contradicts earlier sign-offs.
- "While you're at it…" during an acceptance review.

**Escalation line:** "We've had several new requests this week. That's a good sign the product is getting traction! To protect the launch date, I'd like 20 minutes to re-prioritise together: what's must-have for launch, and what can be phase 2?"
