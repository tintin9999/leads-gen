# Lost-deal follow-ups

A "no" today is often a "yes" in 3–6 months, or a referral to someone else. Losing well is part of selling. These messages keep the door open without begging.

---

## 1. They said no (or chose someone else): send the same day

**Subject:** Re: {{COMPANY}} proposal

Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

Thanks for letting me know, and for being straight with me. I hope {{the launch / the sale}} goes really well.

One small favour, so I can improve: was it mainly price, timing, scope, or fit? Even a one-word answer helps.

If things change, or you need a second pair of eyes on {{checkout / performance / the migration}} later, I'm happy to help.

Best,
{{YOUR_NAME}}

---

## 2. Proposal expired with no reply (after 4 follow-ups)

**Subject:** Closing the file on {{project}}

Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

I haven't heard back, so I'll assume the timing isn't right and close this on my side. No hard feelings at all.

If it comes back on the table, just reply to this email. I'll re-check availability and refresh the options.

{{YOUR_NAME}}

*(This "break-up" email often gets a reply. People don't like loose ends.)*

---

## 3. Nurture: 45–90 days later (send something useful, not "just checking in")

**Subject:** {{Useful thing}} for {{COMPANY}}

Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

Saw {{trigger: your new collection / the funding news / the hiring post for a frontend dev}}. Congrats.

Thought this might be useful: {{one specific thing, e.g., "a 1-page pre-sale speed checklist for Shopify", "how we cut refund failures with scheduled retries at Cultus", or "a 2-min Loom of 3 quick wins I noticed on your new product page"}}.

No agenda. If you want help with it, you know where I am.

{{YOUR_NAME}}

**Good nurture triggers:** funding announcement (`scripts/funding-feed.mjs`), a job post for a frontend or full-stack dev (offer to bridge the gap with a contract), a site redesign or replatform, a sale season approaching, a new market launch, or an outage or bad review mentioning the site or app.

---

## 4. They hired someone else and it's going badly (they come back)

Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

Good to hear from you. Sorry it's been rough. Happy to help.

Before I quote anything on code someone else wrote, I'd suggest a short paid audit ({{price}}, {{days}} days). You'll get a clear picture of what's salvageable and what it'll take, and it's credited if we go ahead. I can start {{date}}.

{{YOUR_NAME}}

*(Never trash the previous developer, even if the code deserves it. Clients notice, and it makes you look small.)*

---

## Log the loss (CRM)

- Stage → `lost` (or `nurture` if it's a timing issue)
- `notes`: the **real** reason, in their words
- `nextDate`: +60 to +90 days for nurture
- Every 10 losses, review the reasons: is the problem price, proof, targeting or speed? Fix one thing.
