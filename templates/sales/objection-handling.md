# Objection handling scripts

**Method (every time):** 1) Acknowledge ("Fair question."). 2) Ask what's behind it ("Compared to what?" / "What's driving that?"). 3) Answer the *real* concern. 4) Offer a choice that keeps the deal moving: less scope, different terms, or a paid first step. **Never** argue, and **never** cut the price without getting something back (see `negotiation-playbook.md`).

---

### "It's too expensive." / "Budget is tight."
> "Fair. Can I ask, compared to what? Another quote, or what you'd budgeted?"

- **Another quote:** "Makes sense to compare. Does their quote include {{the before/after measurement / 30-day support / the checkout work}}? If it's the same scope, take the cheaper one. If not, let's compare like for like."
- **Their budget:** "Then let's make it fit. The Essential option is ₹{{x}} and covers the biggest wins: {{2 items}}. We can add the rest after the sale."
- **Value unclear:** go back to their numbers. "You said mobile is {{N}} sessions a month at {{CR}}%. A 0.1-point lift is about ₹{{v}}/month. Does that match how you think about it?"

### "Let me think about it."
> "Of course. When people say that, it's usually one of three things: price, timing, or whether I'm the right person. Is it one of those? I'd rather sort it out now than leave you guessing."

Then: "If it helps, I can hold the {{date}} start slot until {{day}}."

### "I can get this cheaper on Upwork / Fiverr / from a local agency."
> "You can, and for some jobs you should. If you just need hands to follow a spec, a cheaper option makes sense. What I do differently is own the outcome: I measure before and after, fix what matters, and support it for 30 days. Which matters more to you here?"

### "We'll pay you in equity / revenue share."
> "I appreciate the offer. I don't take equity in place of cash, because I'm a one-person business and rent is paid in rupees. If cash is tight until the round, let's do the {{audit}} now ({{price}}) and plan the build for after you close."

### "Can you do a small free test task first?"
> "I don't do unpaid work, but I understand wanting to lower the risk. Here's what I suggest instead: a paid {{audit/first module}} for {{price}}, with a clear deliverable in {{N}} days. It's credited if we continue. If you don't like it, you've risked very little."

(If it's a marketplace interview with a ≤ 60-minute exercise on *their* platform/IDE, that's normal. Never run their code on your own machine: memo 11, safe-sandbox SOP.)

### "Can you start now? We'll sign the contract later."
> "I can start tomorrow. The agreement is one page and takes two minutes to sign, and the deposit invoice goes out with it. As soon as both are done, I'm on it."

### "Pay after launch / once we get funding / when the client pays us."
> "I understand cash flow. My terms are 50% to start and 50% on delivery, and that's what lets me commit dates. If upfront is the issue, we can split it into smaller milestones so each payment is smaller."

### "Send your portfolio / CV first."
Send **2–3 targeted links**, not everything:
- Cultus case study (commerce/payments) **or** the relevant spec project
- A one-paragraph Deribit/Twinntax summary (no confidential details)
- Your site

> "Here's the most relevant work. Happy to walk you through how I'd approach yours in 20 minutes: {{CAL_LINK}}."

### "Do you have reviews / references?"
> "I'm new to freelancing under my own name, but not new to client work: four years on client engagements like Deribit and a tax-filing platform used by 100,000+ people. Happy to put you in touch with {{a former colleague / Cultus brand partner}} as a reference. And the first step is a small paid audit, so the risk is low."

### "Can you guarantee results?" / "Guarantee a 20% conversion lift?"
> "I guarantee the deliverables and the technical targets: LCP, test coverage, zero critical findings. Revenue also depends on traffic, pricing and offers, which I don't control. If I miss an agreed technical target, I keep working until I hit it, at no extra cost."

### "Can we do hourly instead?"
> "For well-defined work, fixed price protects you from overruns. For open-ended work, a weekly rate with a Friday demo works well. You see progress every week and can stop anytime. Which fits better?"

### "Why should we hire a freelancer and not an agency?"
> "With an agency you usually get a project manager plus whoever is free. With me you get the engineer who built Cultus's payments and Shopify integration, directly, with no hand-offs. For larger scopes I bring in vetted specialists and stay accountable."

### "We need you to sign our NDA first."
> "Sure, send it over. I'll read it today. If it's one-way, I'll ask for mutual terms." (Check for hidden non-competes, IP grabs and penalty clauses: `templates/contracts/red-flag-clauses.md`.)

### "Our last developer left halfway. Can you take over?"
> "Yes, if we start with a paid code audit ({{price}}, {{days}} days). I won't quote a fixed price for someone else's half-built code without seeing it. The audit tells us what's salvageable."

### "Can you be available on WhatsApp all the time?"
> "I reply within {{2 working hours}} on weekdays, {{10–7 IST}}. Urgent production issues are covered by the care plan, which includes a faster response."

---

**Log it:** after every lost or stalled deal, write the real objection in the CRM `notes`. After 10 deals, the pattern tells you what to fix: offer, price, proof, or targeting.
