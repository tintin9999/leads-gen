The plan: freelancer first, business later

You do not have to become a business this week. You start as a freelancer on one portal, Upwork. The business parts come later, in small steps, when real money asks for them.

What one early week can look like#

It is your third week. On Monday you send three short proposals on Upwork.

On Wednesday a founder called Maya replies. She is a made-up client. Her app has a login bug and a payment bug.

On Thursday she hires you for a fixed price of $300. Upwork holds her money before you start.

You fix the bugs in four days. She approves the work and leaves a five-star review.

Look at what you did not need. You had no company, no tax number and no contract of your own.

Upwork wrote the contract. Upwork made the invoice. Upwork held the money safely until the work was done.

That is why Upwork is a good first step for someone with work experience and no business experience.

The three stages#

The whole plan has three stages. You only work on one stage at a time.

StageYour one goalIt ends when
1. Upwork firstThree paid jobs with five-star reviewsYou have about five good reviews
2. Raise your rateBetter pay, plus a few clients from outside UpworkYou earn about ₹1 lakh a month for three months
3. Act like a businessYour own contracts, invoices and monthly clientsIt does not end

Stage 1 takes about eight weeks for most people. It can be faster or slower. Both are normal.

Why this order? Reviews come first because they make every later step easier.

A profile with five good reviews wins jobs at a higher price. It also gives you true stories to tell clients outside Upwork.

You are in Stage 1 now. The other two stages have their own pages, and they can wait.

What Stage 1 asks from you#

Stage 1 has a simple daily shape. You give Upwork about two hours every working day.

  1. Look at new job posts for 20 minutes, three times a day.
  2. Pick two or three posts that fit what you sell.
  3. Write a short, careful proposal for each one.
  4. Reply fast when a client answers.

The rest of your day is for doing paid jobs when you have them. Until then, use it for small proof pieces and for Cultus.

Cultus gets a fixed block of hours each week, agreed with your co-founders. That stops the two from eating each other.

Your first jobs will be small. A small job with a happy client is worth more than a big job right now.

Reviews are the point of Stage 1. Clients on Upwork trust reviews more than anything you write about yourself.

What you can ignore for now#

New freelancers often freeze because the list of "business things" looks endless. Most of that list is not needed in Stage 1.

  • A company. You do not need one. In Indian law you can earn as a person working alone.
  • A registration for the tax on services (GST). It becomes a must only above ₹20 lakh of yearly income.
  • Your own contracts. Upwork's terms cover every Upwork job.
  • Your own invoices. Upwork creates them for you.
  • A bigger website. Your site at parthkulkarni.dev is already enough.
  • A stream of emails to strangers. Five a week is plenty. See the last heading on this page.

Each of these has a page under "For later" on the map. Open that page when the moment arrives, and not before.

A few small setups do matter before your first job. There are six of them, and step 3 covers each one.

What is normal in the first weeks#

Upwork can feel scary at first. Knowing what is normal makes it easier.

  • Most proposals get no answer. That is true even for good freelancers.
  • A new profile with no reviews gets fewer replies than an old one. The first job is the hardest to win.
  • Many people send 20 to 40 proposals before the first job. Treat that as the cost of entry.
  • Some job posts are fake or unsafe. A one-minute check catches almost all of them.

None of this means you are doing it wrong. It means you are at the start.

If a real problem comes up, there is a page for it under "Fix a problem". You do not have to read those pages in advance.

The slow backup#

Upwork is your main road. You also keep one small side road open from week 1.

Each week you send five personal messages or emails. You start with people who already know your work.

This may bring one lucky client, or nothing for months. Both results are fine. It costs you 20 minutes a day.

Step 11 shows how to do it without letting it eat your Upwork time.

Do this now#

  • Pick your daily two-hour Upwork slot and put it in your calendar as a repeating event.
  • Write down one number: how many months your savings can cover with no income.
  • Tell your Cultus co-founders how many hours a week you will give Cultus from now on.
