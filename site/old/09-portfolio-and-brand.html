Case study format

  1. Title = outcome. "Keeping 12 brands' Shopify inventory in sync with a marketplace — without overselling."
  2. Context — who the client is, in two sentences.
  3. Problem — in business terms (lost orders, manual work, risk), then the technical cause.
  4. Constraints — budget, timeline, legacy systems, team size.
  5. Approach — 3–5 key decisions, each with the trade-off. One architecture diagram.
  6. Results — numbers, before/after, what happened next.
  7. Quote — one sentence from the client or a colleague.
  8. Call to action — "Have a similar problem? Book a 20-minute call."

Keep three lengths of every case study: the full page (600–1,000 words), a three-bullet version for proposals, and a one-liner for DMs ("I built the Shopify Sales Channel app behind Cultus — 12 brands syncing inventory every minute").

NDA and confidentiality. Client work you did as a Noovosoft employee is covered by your employment terms and the client's agreements. Safe default: name the client only as it appears on your résumé, describe your approach and publicly known facts, and never share code, screenshots of internal tools, data, or internal metrics. When unsure, anonymise ("a top crypto derivatives exchange"). See Risks & Scams.

Two spec projects that double as lead magnets

A spec project is something you build without a client to prove a specific offer. Choose ones that also start conversations. Briefs are in spec-projects.md.

The Indian D2C Store Speed Report (Track A)

Run scripts/site-audit.mjs with PageSpeed on ~100 Indian D2C stores. Publish aggregate findings (median mobile LCP, top slow apps, image weight, checkout stacks) without shaming individual brands. Then send each brand its own results privately.

Effort: 2–3 days. Payoff: a LinkedIn post series, a site page, and a reason to message 100 brands.

Supabase Production-Readiness Kit (Track B)

An open-source checklist plus a small CLI that founders run against their own project: RLS enabled on every table, policies tested, storage buckets private, service-role key never in the client, auth redirect URLs locked, backups on. Ship a demo repo with RLS tests in CI.

Effort: 4–5 days. Payoff: credibility for B1, GitHub stars, posts for builder communities.

Later (month 2–3): two more spec projects
  • Admin dashboard starter (B2): Next.js + Supabase + shadcn/ui with RBAC, approval flow, audit log, CSV export, tests. Live demo with seeded data.
  • Angular → React strangler demo (B3): an AngularJS app with React islands mounted per route, shared state bridge, Vitest suite, and a write-up of the migration plan.
Only audit what you're allowed to. Public PageSpeed tests of homepages are fine. Probing someone else's app or database for security holes without written permission is not, even with good intentions. For B1 teardowns, ask first or use your own demo apps.

LinkedIn as a sales asset

For B2B services in India, LinkedIn is where buyers check you out after any first contact. Full copy in linkedin-profile.md.

  • Headline: outcome + buyers + proof ("Product engineer for commerce & internal tools · Built Cultus · Ex-Deribit (Coinbase) back office").
  • Banner: one line of offer, one line of proof, your site URL.
  • About: problem → proof → offers → call to action (not a résumé).
  • Featured: Cultus case study, the speed report, booking link.
  • Experience: add "Independent product engineer" as current; rewrite Noovosoft bullets as outcomes.
  • Create a free LinkedIn Service Page (category: web development / software development) so buyers can send you proposal requests.
  • Ask five people for recommendations: ex-manager, a senior colleague, two mentees, a Cultus brand partner.
  • Turn on "Providing services"; don't use the #OpenToWork photo frame (it signals job-seeking).
How Service Page requests work. Clients can send a request for proposal for free; requests are matched by service category and locale and may also be sent to other providers, with a cap on responses. Reply within 24 hours and reference details from the request.
