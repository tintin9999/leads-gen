Bottom line. You do not need to register a company to start. The day you invoice a client you are a sole proprietor: your PAN is the business. Week-1 setup costs about ₹15k–30k (mostly health insurance) and 8–12 hours: a separate bank account, Udyam registration (free), Maharashtra PTEC (₹2,500/yr), individual health cover plus a super top-up, a domain and professional email, and an invoice series with a simple books sheet. Register for GST only when your all-India turnover is heading past ₹20L in the financial year, or when Indian business clients need GST invoices. Then file the LUT the same week. Look at an LLP or Pvt Ltd only at around ₹60–75L in receipts, when you hire, or when an enterprise client insists on a company. Facts below are as of Nov 2026.

What you legally are on day 1

A sole proprietorship is not a separate legal person. It is you, trading. No registration creates it. You get the following:

  • Identity: your PAN. Clients deduct TDS against it, payment platforms run KYC on it, and your ITR reports the business on it.
  • Tax: profits are taxed in your own return as "profits and gains of business or profession". You can use the presumptive scheme for professionals (s.58 of the Income-tax Act 2025, formerly 44ADA). See Tax & Money.
  • Liability: unlimited. If a client sues, your personal assets are exposed. Your shield is contractual: a liability cap, no consequential damages and clear acceptance criteria. See Contracts.
  • Name: you can trade under your own name or a trade name. A trade name only becomes "official" through documents that carry it, such as Udyam, GST and the bank account.
You already have the essentials. PAN, Aadhaar linked to your mobile, a bank account and a laptop. Everything else below is about getting paid faster, getting taxed less and looking like a vendor a CFO can approve.

Tax is not a reason to incorporate below ₹75L. With s.58 and the new regime, a sole proprietor pays about ₹0 tax on ₹24L gross and about 9.8% on ₹75L. Upgrade triggers (review every quarter)

  1. Receipts past ~₹60L and rising toward ₹75L. Above ₹75L the presumptive scheme ends, so you need books, possibly an audit, and a CA-led structure decision.
  2. You start reselling other people's work. Heavy subcontracting means real expenses above 50% of receipts, and presumptive taxation stops fitting.
  3. Hiring an employee or two or more regular subcontractors.
  4. Enterprise or US clients require a company vendor, or ask for liability or insurance levels you would not carry personally.
  5. A partner joins (LLP) or you build a product or raise money (Pvt Ltd).

Banking: separate the money on day 1

Phase 1 (now): a dedicated savings account

  • Use one savings account, existing or a new zero-balance account, only for business: every client payment lands here and every business expense leaves from here. This keeps your gross receipts provable, makes your CA's work cheap and makes the later switch to GST painless.
  • Create a UPI ID on this account for small Indian payments, and print a QR for in-person work.
  • Open a tax-reserve sub-account, sweep FD or second account, and move a fixed percentage into it on every receipt. See Money routine.
  • Foreign client money can land in a savings account in your name through the payment rails in Tax & Money.

Phase 2 (once you have Udyam plus GST or PTEC, or regular volume): a current account

  • A current account in the proprietorship's name. Under RBI KYC rules, banks ask a proprietor for two proofs that the business exists. Commonly accepted: Udyam certificate, GST certificate, professional tax (PTEC) certificate, Shops & Establishment intimation, IEC, or a utility bill in the business name.
  • Trade-offs: current accounts pay no interest and usually need an average balance (about ₹10k–25k), but they handle high-volume business flows without the bank raising questions.
  • IEC (₹500 on DGFT) is generally not needed by service exporters unless you claim Foreign Trade Policy benefits. Get it only if a bank or programme insists.

How Indian clients pay you

  • Default: NEFT/IMPS/RTGS to the business account. It is free, traceable and works with TDS.
  • Small or one-off: UPI.
  • Card or "pay link" requested: a Razorpay payment link (about 2% + GST). Price the fee in or ask the client to bear it.
  • Designate (or open) the business-only account and move all client payments there
  • Create a business UPI ID on it
  • Open a tax-reserve sub-account / sweep FD
  • Calendar reminder: open a current account once Udyam + PTEC/GST are done

Udyam (MSME) registration: free leverage over late payers

What: an Aadhaar-based, self-declared registration at udyamregistration.gov.in. It is free, takes about 20–30 minutes and produces a certificate with a permanent Udyam number. You qualify as micro if your investment is ≤ ₹2.5 Cr and turnover ≤ ₹10 Cr (revised limits from 1 April 2025, S.O. 1364(E)). For activity, pick a computer-programming NIC code (custom software development under group 6201). The portal lists the options.

Use only the .gov.in portal. Lookalike sites charge ₹1,000–3,000 to "file" Udyam for you, and some harvest Aadhaar data. The official registration is free and needs no agent.

GST: register when it pays, not before

GST is the biggest setup decision because it brings recurring filings. Work through the questions in order:

  1. Will all-India aggregate turnover pass ₹20L this financial year (April–March)? Count every client, foreign and domestic, excluding GST itself. If yes: registration is mandatory within 30 days of crossing. Apply once you cross about ₹15L so the GSTIN exists before you hit ₹20L.
  2. Do Indian business clients ask for a GST invoice? Registered companies can claim your 18% back as input tax credit, so it costs them nothing net, and some procurement systems only onboard GST-registered vendors. If several do, register voluntarily.
  3. Are you below ₹20L with foreign clients only? The prevailing reading is that Notification 10/2017-Integrated Tax exempts inter-state service suppliers below the threshold, and exports of services count as inter-state supplies. On that reading registration is not mandatory. A minority view says zero-rated suppliers must register. Conservative path: stay unregistered under about ₹15L, keep FIRAs and invoices clean, have your CA confirm, and register as you approach ₹20L.
  4. Are most domestic clients small and unregistered (e.g. tiny D2C brands)? Registering early makes you 18% dearer to them, so don't register before you must.
Voluntary registration: prosCons
  • Credibility with Indian companies; passes vendor onboarding
  • Input tax credit on laptops, monitors, coworking and Indian SaaS. Exporters under LUT can claim it back as a refund
  • Clean zero-rated exports under LUT
  • No scramble when you cross ₹20L mid-year
  • Quarterly GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B (QRMP), filed even when nil, with late fees if missed
  • CA fees of about ₹10k–30k/yr if you don't file yourself
  • Reverse charge on imported services, such as foreign SaaS bought for the business (ask your CA)
  • 18% on top for unregistered Indian clients
  • Cancelling later has its own process

How to register (when the time comes)

  1. gst.gov.in → Services → Registration → New Registration (REG-01). Part A: PAN, mobile and email OTPs, which give you a TRN.
  2. Part B: business details; principal place of business (home is fine), with an electricity bill plus a rent agreement or the owner's consent/NOC if you don't own it; a photo; bank proof; activity "IT design & development services" (SAC 998314).
  3. Opt for Aadhaar authentication. Approval usually takes about 7 working days. Without it you may face a physical verification.
  4. Choose QRMP (quarterly returns) and immediately file the LUT (RFD-11) so foreign invoices go out at 0%. See GST in practice.

Maharashtra professional tax (PTEC)

  • Who: self-employed professionals in Maharashtra enrol for a PTEC (Enrolment Certificate) on mahagst.gov.in.
  • How much: ₹2,500 per year, flat. There is no return to file for PTEC.
  • When: if you enrol after 31 May, the first payment is due within a month of enrolment. After that it is due by 30 June each year. A February 2026 amendment reportedly moved this to 15 June, so check the MahaGST portal. Late payment attracts interest of about 1.25% per month.
  • Bonus: the PTEC certificate is one of the two business proofs a bank accepts for a current account.
  • Under the presumptive scheme you can't deduct it separately. Pay it for compliance, not for any tax benefit.
  • Enrol for PTEC on mahagst.gov.in and pay ₹2,500 (save challan)
  • Calendar: annual PTEC payment (June) — confirm 15 vs 30 June on the portal

Safety net: you just lost your employer's

Leaving Noovosoft in September 2026 ended three things you probably did not think about: group health cover, employer PF contributions and a predictable salary. Replace them deliberately.

1. Health insurance (do this week)

If you didn't migrate the group policy before leaving, buy an individual policy now. IRDAI rules let you migrate group cover to an individual policy with waiting-period credit on the existing sum insured, but you normally have to apply before cover lapses. Suggested shape: ₹10L base + ₹50L super top-up with a ₹10L deductible. Ballpark at your age in Pune is about ₹7k–12k for the base and ₹2k–4k for the top-up per year. Individual health and life policies have been GST-free since 22 Sept 2025. Check for no room-rent cap, restoration, pre-existing-disease waiting of 3 years or less, Pune network hospitals and the claim-settlement record.

2. Personal accident + disability

If you are self-employed and can't work, your income stops. A ₹25–50L personal accident cover with temporary and permanent disability benefits costs about ₹1k–3k/yr.

3. Term life (only if someone depends on you)

If parents or a partner rely on your income, or you have loans, buy pure term cover of about 10–15× annual expenses (₹1 Cr is about ₹8k–14k/yr for a young non-smoker). Never ULIP or endowment.

4. Emergency fund / runway

6 months of personal expenses + 3 months of business costs, kept in a liquid FD or sweep account and separate from operating money. Model it in Rate, Tax & Runway.

5. EPF: leave it unless desperate

Your PF balance stays put. Under the reforms announced in October 2025, up to 75% can be withdrawn right after leaving, and the rest after 12 months of unemployment (check the EPFO site for the version in force). You have under 5 years of service, so a withdrawal is taxable. Interest credited after you stop being an employee is generally taxable too. If you take a salaried role later, transfer the balance.

6. Long-term saving

The new tax regime gives no deduction for PPF or NPS contributions you make yourself. PPF interest is still tax-free, though. Once income stabilises, automate a monthly SIP or PPF transfer straight after the tax reserve ("pay yourself first").

  • Buy individual health policy (₹10L base) + super top-up (₹50L, ₹10L deductible)
  • Buy personal accident cover with disability benefit
  • Decide on term cover (only if dependants/loans)
  • Fund the emergency account; record runway months in the CRM dashboard notes
  • Log into EPFO (UAN), confirm exit date is marked, nominee is updated — do not withdraw by default
