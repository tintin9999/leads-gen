  2. Price — package the outcome into fixed-scope offers with three options. → 04 Pricing

Operating principles

1 · Warm before cold

People who already trust you convert 5–10× better. Exhaust the warm network before you scale outreach to strangers.

2 · Sell outcomes, price packages

"Mobile LCP under 2.5 s in 10 days for ₹45k" beats "React dev, ₹1,500/hr."

3 · Diagnose before you prescribe

Paid audits and discovery sprints turn strangers into clients with low risk on both sides.

4 · No deposit, no start

Signed SOW + deposit received = kickoff. No exceptions for "urgent" work. Urgency is when it matters most.

5 · Always be prospecting

Keep at least 20% of your week on pipeline, even when busy, or you'll hit a famine 6 weeks after the feast.

6 · Write it down

Every conversation goes in the CRM with a next action and a date. Memory is not a system.

7 · Raise prices on a schedule

Increase rates 10–20% for new clients after every 3 wins, or when you're booked more than 6 weeks out.

8 · Protect the downside

Six months' runway, no client above 50% of revenue, contracts for everything, a sandbox for untrusted code.

9 · Every project → four assets

Case study, testimonial, referral ask, retainer offer. Ask for all four at handover, while the client is happiest.

Business glossary (plain English)

Sales & marketing terms
TermMeaning
ICP (ideal customer profile)A precise description of the client you serve best: industry, size, stage, stack, trigger events. You write messages for one ICP at a time.
PositioningWhat you're known for and for whom. Good positioning makes you the obvious choice for a narrow problem.
Offer / productized serviceA packaged service with a fixed scope, timeline and price, e.g., "Store Speed Sprint — ₹45k, 10 days".
Lead → prospect → opportunityLead: a name that might fit. Prospect: qualified fit you've contacted. Opportunity/deal: a real conversation about a project.
PipelineAll open deals by stage. Weighted pipeline = Σ (deal value × win probability).
Warm / cold outreachWarm: they know you or a mutual contact. Cold: they don't. Warm converts far better.
Trigger / signalAn event that creates a need now: funding round, new hire posting, replatforming, bad reviews, slow site, launch.
Discovery callA 20–30 minute call to understand the problem, the impact, the budget and the decision process before proposing anything.
QualificationDeciding whether a lead is worth pursuing: fit, budget, authority, timeline, ability to pay.
ProposalA written recommendation with scope, options, price and next steps. Not a quote — it sells.
AnchoringShowing a higher-priced option first so the recommended option feels reasonable.
Conversion ratePercent moving from one stage to the next (e.g., calls → proposals).
CAC / LTVCost (mostly your time) to acquire a client / total revenue from a client over the relationship.
Case study / testimonial / referralA written story with numbers / a quote from the client / an introduction to a new client from an existing one.
Delivery & contract terms
TermMeaning
MSA (master services agreement)The umbrella contract: IP, payment, liability, confidentiality. Signed once per client.
SOW (statement of work)Per-project document: scope, deliverables, acceptance criteria, timeline, price, milestones.
Change request (CR)A written, priced change to the SOW. Your defence against scope creep.
Scope creepUnpaid growth of the work ("small tweak", "while you're at it").
Acceptance criteria / UATThe agreed test of "done" / the client's user-acceptance testing window.
Deposit / milestone billingUpfront payment before work / staged payments tied to deliverables.
Net 7 / Net 30Invoice due 7 / 30 days after the invoice date.
Kill feePayment owed if the client cancels mid-project.
Retainer / care planMonthly fee for a reserved block of availability or ongoing maintenance.
UtilizationBillable hours ÷ available hours. 50–65% is healthy for a solo freelancer; the rest is selling and admin.
Effective hourly rateProject fee ÷ hours you actually spent. The real measure of a fixed-price deal.
Limitation of liabilityA cap on how much you can owe if something goes wrong, usually the fees paid.
India tax & money terms
TermMeaning
Presumptive taxation (s.58, formerly 44ADA)Professionals with receipts within the limit can declare 50% of gross receipts as taxable income without detailed books. Huge simplification and usually a big tax saving.
New tax regimeDefault slabs with few deductions. Income up to ₹12L effectively tax-free via rebate.
Advance taxPaying income tax during the year. Under presumptive taxation, generally one instalment by 15 March.
TDSTax deducted at source: Indian business clients may withhold 10% (professional) or 2% (technical) of your fee and deposit it against your PAN. You claim it back in your return.
GST, GSTIN, SACGoods and Services Tax (18% on IT services), your GST registration number, and the service code on invoices (IT design & development: 998314).
Export of services / zero-ratedServices to a client abroad, paid in foreign currency: no GST is charged.
LUTLetter of Undertaking: filed annually by GST-registered exporters to invoice foreign clients without paying IGST.
FIRA / FIRC / e-BRCBank or platform documents proving a foreign payment came in. Keep them for tax and GST.
Purpose codeRBI code telling the bank why money arrived (software services).
Udyam / MSMEFree government registration as a micro enterprise. Gives statutory protection against late payments by Indian buyers.
W-8BENUS form a US client may ask for, certifying you're a non-US person.
ITR-4 / ITR-3Income-tax return forms: ITR-4 for presumptive income, ITR-3 for regular business income.
