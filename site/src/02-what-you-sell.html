A client decides in a few seconds whether you are the right person. One clear sentence about what you fix does more than a long list of skills. This page gives you that sentence.

Two ways to say the same skills

Here are two opening lines for the same person: you.

Line A: "React and Next.js developer with 4 years of experience. Available for work."

Line B: "I fix and finish web apps built with Next.js, React and Supabase."

Line A is true, and thousands of profiles say the same thing. A client cannot tell you apart, so they compare on price.

Line B names a problem. A founder with a half-working app reads it and thinks, "that is my situation".

The general rule is simple. Say what you fix and for whom. Keep the list of tools for later in the profile.

Your sentence

Use this as your one sentence on Upwork:

I fix and finish web apps built with Next.js, React and Supabase.

Behind it sit four kinds of job. Each one is a real search that clients make.

  • Login and data safety. Sign-in that breaks, and data that the wrong user can see.
  • Payments. Checkout bugs, failed payments and refunds, with Stripe or Razorpay.
  • Admin screens. The inside pages a team uses to run its work.
  • Half-built apps. Apps made quickly with an AI app builder that now need to work for real users.

An AI app builder is a tool that writes an app from a text request. Lovable, Bolt and v0 are common ones.

Many founders now build a first version this way. Then login, payments or data safety break, and they look for a developer.

The proof behind the sentence

A client has a few quiet fears about any new freelancer. Each of your past projects answers one of them.

The client's fearYour proof
"Can he build a whole product?"Cultus: a live marketplace you built end to end
"Will he break my payments?"Cultus checkout, with 115 test files on the money paths
"Can he work in a serious codebase?"Admin tools for Deribit, owned by Coinbase
"Can he handle complex forms?"A tax module used by 100,000+ people in Belgium
"Can he rescue messy code?"The Bro bot: rewritten and grown to 2.4 million members

You do not need all five in one message. Pick the one that matches the job in front of you.

For a payment bug, lead with Cultus checkout. For a messy app, lead with the Bro rewrite.

Who buys this on Upwork

Most buyers of this service are small. Think of a founder, or a team of two to ten people.

They are mostly in the US, the UK, Europe and Australia. They have a product that half works and a date they must hit.

Their job posts sound like this:

  • "Fix login and Stripe checkout bugs in my Next.js + Supabase app"
  • "Need a developer to finish my Lovable app and make it secure"
  • "Build an admin dashboard for our orders in React"

These posts are a good match for you. The work is clear, the size is small, and your proof fits.

What you do not sell yet

Saying no to some work makes the rest of your profile stronger. For now, leave these out.

  • Websites on WordPress or Wix, logos and phone apps. They are not your strength.
  • Shopify apps. You are good at this. It becomes a second Upwork profile after your first reviews.
  • Work for Indian brands. That is a Stage 2 road, with its own page under "For later".

You can still take a good job outside your sentence if one comes. You just do not advertise it.

One sentence is not a cage. It is the first thing people remember about you, so make it easy to repeat.

Where the sentence goes

You will use the same sentence in three places. Using it everywhere makes you easy to remember.

  • Your Upwork profile. It shapes your title and the first line of your overview. Step 5 shows how.
  • Your proposals. The first line names the client's problem. The second line is your sentence.
  • Your website and LinkedIn. One line at the top, so every visitor reads the same message.

Do not write a new pitch for each place. Repeating one sentence feels boring to you, and it works on clients.

Say it in your own words

The sentence must sound like you. Change the words until you can say it without reading.

  1. Write the sentence on paper in your own words.
  2. Say it aloud to one friend who is not a developer.
  3. Ask them to repeat it back an hour later.
  4. If they can, keep it. If they cannot, make it shorter.

A friend who can repeat your sentence can also pass your name on. That is how many freelancers get their second client.
