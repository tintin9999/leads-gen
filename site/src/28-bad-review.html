A bad review hurts most in the first week you see it. It rarely ends a profile. This page shows a calm public reply, what Upwork lets you do, and how to recover with your next three jobs.

A 3-star review after a rushed job

Your fourth job is a $200 fixed-price fix. The client wants the data-access rules in Supabase tightened in two days. You agree, though two days is tight.

You deliver on time. A day later the client finds that one admin page no longer loads. You fix it within hours, but the client is annoyed.

The review: 3 stars. "Knows Supabase, but the work felt rushed. I found a bug after delivery."

You post this public reply under the review:

Thank you for the honest feedback. The admin page bug was my miss, and I fixed it the same day. I now test every admin page before handover, not only the changed ones.

That reply is for future clients, not for this one. It shows that you take blame calmly and learn.

Never argue, blame the client, or explain at length. A defensive reply hurts more than the review.

What Upwork lets you do

As of November 2026, these are your options. Use them in this order.

  1. Wait one day before you do anything. Read the review again when you are calm.
  2. Reply publicly. Open the closed contract and find the client's feedback. You get one reply, within 28 days of the contract ending, and you cannot edit it later.
  3. If the client was happy in the chat, ask once, politely, whether they would update the feedback.
  4. If they agree, open the contract and click Enable client to change feedback. They then have 14 days to change it.
  5. Report the review only if it breaks Upwork's rules. Use the report option next to the feedback.

Ask for a change only once, and never offer money or free work for it. That breaks Upwork's rules and can cost you the account.

Thanks again for working with me. You mentioned you were happy once the fix was in. If you feel the review should reflect that, I've enabled a change, with no pressure at all.

Upwork removes feedback only when it breaks the Terms of Service. Examples are threats, or a review used to demand free work. An honest opinion stays, even if you think it is unfair.

Upwork says it reviews a report within about seven working days, and that removal is rare.

Freelancers with the Top Rated badge have reportedly been able to remove one feedback now and then. The current conditions are not on a page we could confirm. Check the current rule on Upwork's help page when you reach Top Rated.

How one bad result moves an early score

Upwork shows a percentage on your profile, called the Job Success Score. It appears after two finished contracts with two different clients.

In outline, Upwork takes good outcomes minus bad outcomes, and divides by all outcomes. Larger jobs count more, and private feedback counts too.

So early on, one bad outcome weighs a lot. The numbers below use the outline only, with equal jobs.

Three good jobs, one bad (if Upwork counts it as bad)
(3 good − 1 bad) ÷ 4 outcomes     = 2 ÷ 4  = 50%

After three more good jobs
(6 good − 1 bad) ÷ 7 outcomes     = 5 ÷ 7  = 71%

After six more good jobs
(9 good − 1 bad) ÷ 10 outcomes    = 8 ÷ 10 = 80%

The real score uses more detail than this, so your number will differ. The shape is what matters: each good job dilutes the bad one.

A 3-star public review may not count as a bad outcome at all. Upwork leans on the client's private feedback, which you never see.

The next three jobs to take

Your next three jobs have one goal: three clean five-star finishes. Pick them for safety, not money.

  • Small and clear. Fixed price, $150 to $400, with a named result and a way to check it.
  • Inside your service. Login, Supabase data rules, payments or an admin page. Nothing new to learn.
  • Kind clients. Clients whose past reviews of freelancers are specific and fair.
  • Time to spare. Promise four days where you think it takes two.

The 2-minute check in Pick jobs worth applying to finds these. Keep your rate at $35 to $40 an hour meanwhile.

Warning signs to act on before a job ends

Most bad reviews were visible days before. Act on these signs while the contract is still open.

  • The client goes quiet for two days, or replies get short.
  • The client says "I thought it would also…" about something not agreed.
  • You will miss a date, even by one day.
  • The client finds a bug you did not test for.

When you see one, offer a short call. Write down what "done" means, in the chat, and agree it.

Tell bad news early and with a new date. I am stuck or will miss the deadline has the words.

Never end a contract in the middle of a disagreement. Settle it first, even if that means a small partial refund.

Do this now

  • If you have a bad review, wait one day, then post a three-sentence reply.
  • Write one change to your work routine that the review taught you.
  • Save two small, clear jobs from your searches for your next proposals.
  • Add "test every page before handover" to your delivery checklist.
