India's tax on services is called Goods and Services Tax (GST). For now, you most likely do not need to register for it. This page shows the one line that changes that answer, and what changes when you cross it.

Three cases

Case 1: Upwork and foreign clients only, ₹9 lakh a year. You work for Maya, a made-up founder in the US, and a few others like her. All of it comes through Upwork, paid in dollars.

Your receipts this year                    ₹9,00,000
Registration line                         ₹20,00,000
₹9 lakh is below ₹20 lakh                → no need to register
GST you charge clients                   = ₹0
GST Upwork adds on its fee, on a $1,000 job:
  Upwork fee:  10% of $1,000              =   $100
  GST on the fee:  18% of $100            =    $18

Nothing changes for you. The $18 is a cost you already see on each Upwork job.

Case 2: an Indian business client asks for a tax invoice. Asha, a made-up owner of the Pune brand Indigo Thread, wants a ₹60,000 job. Her accountant asks for a GST invoice, a bill that shows GST, so the company can claim the tax back.

If you are not registered:
  your invoice                             ₹60,000
  GST on it                              =      ₹0
  Asha pays                              = ₹60,000
If you were registered:
  your invoice                             ₹60,000
  GST:  18% of ₹60,000                   = ₹10,800
  Asha pays                              = ₹70,800
  Asha claims back from the government   − ₹10,800
  Asha's real cost                       = ₹60,000

You do not have to register. Tell her you are below the line and send a plain invoice with your PAN. Most small clients accept this.

If several clients insist, registering early may be worth it.

Case 3: total receipts pass ₹20 lakh. In a good year, by December you have received ₹21 lakh from all clients together.

Receipts, April to December               ₹21,00,000
Registration line                         ₹20,00,000
₹21 lakh is above ₹20 lakh                → must register
Deadline:                                   within 30 days of crossing

Now you must register. Better still, apply when you reach about ₹15 lakh, so your number is ready in time.

The ₹20 lakh line

You must register once your total receipts in a tax year, April to March, pass ₹20 lakh. This is the rule as of November 2026.

Count every client: Upwork, foreign and Indian. Do not count GST itself. Below the line, you charge no GST, and you must not show GST on an invoice.

When you register, you get a 15-character tax number (GSTIN). It goes on every invoice after that.

Foreign clients pay 0%

Once registered, you charge Indian clients 18% on top of your price. You charge foreign clients 0%, because work for them, paid in foreign money, counts as an export.

To charge 0%, you file a free online paper each year called a Letter of Undertaking (LUT). It is a promise that your foreign work is exports. File it before your first foreign invoice of each April-to-March year.

Keep the bank's proof of each foreign payment. It shows the money really came from abroad. The page Getting paid from abroad explains it.

The 18% Upwork adds

Upwork charges you 18% GST on its own fee and on Connects, the tokens you spend to apply. This happens because you have no GSTIN yet.

It stops once you give Upwork your GSTIN in its tax settings. You then handle that tax in your own returns, and can usually claim it back. Ask a chartered accountant about this one.

On ₹9 lakh a year of Upwork work at a 10% fee, this tax is about ₹18,000. That alone is not a reason to register.

One honest open question

Experts disagree about one case: someone below ₹20 lakh who works only for foreign clients.

Most guides, and the payment services, say registration is not needed. A smaller group says exporters must register to claim the 0% rate.

The careful path: stay unregistered while you are well below the line. Keep invoices and payment proofs clean. Ask a chartered accountant to confirm once, in writing, and register as you near ₹20 lakh.

What registering costs in effort

Registration itself is free on gst.gov.in and takes about a week. The cost is the work after it.

  • A sales return every three months, filed even if it shows nothing.
  • A payment return every three months, also filed even if empty.
  • Late fees if you miss one.
  • About 1 hour a month of record keeping, or ₹10,000–30,000 a year for a chartered accountant to file for you.

That is why you register when you must, or when it clearly pays, and not before.

Do this now

  • Add up your receipts since April. Write the total in your money sheet.
  • Set a note: "Apply for GST at ₹15 lakh."
  • If an Indian client asks for a GST invoice, send a plain invoice with your PAN and say you are below the line.
  • Keep the payment proof for every foreign payment.
