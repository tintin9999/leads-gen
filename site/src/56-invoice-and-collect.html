With a direct client, nobody sends the bill for you. You need an invoice that is correct, sent on the right day, and a calm routine if the money is late. This page shows one real invoice, line by line.

Asha's deposit invoice, field by field

Asha runs "Indigo Thread", a made-up Pune clothing brand on Shopify. On Thursday 12 November she says yes to the Store Speed Sprint at ₹48,000. That day you send the invoice for the first half (the deposit).

You are not registered for service tax (GST), so the invoice has no tax line. The number starts with your initials (PK). Here is every field:

  • Title: Invoice
  • Invoice number: PK/26-27/001
  • Invoice date: 12 November 2026
  • Due date: 19 November 2026 (7 days)
  • From: Parth Kulkarni, Pune, Maharashtra. PAN: your PAN.
  • To: Indigo Thread, attention Asha, with the business address she gives you.
  • Description: Store Speed Sprint, 50% deposit of the ₹48,000 fixed fee, as per the agreement dated 12 November 2026.
  • Amount: ₹24,000
  • Total due: ₹24,000 (Rupees twenty-four thousand only)
  • Tax note: Not registered under GST. No GST charged.
  • How to pay: bank transfer to your account name, bank, account number and the bank's branch code (IFSC), or UPI to your UPI ID.

That is the whole thing. A clear invoice gets paid faster, because her accountant has nothing to ask.

Two taxes that change the numbers

Service tax. India's tax on services is the Goods and Services Tax (GST). Once you register for it, you must add 18% to every Indian invoice.

Fee for the deposit          = ₹24,000
GST at 18%                   = ₹24,000 × 0.18 = ₹4,320
Total on the invoice         = ₹24,000 + ₹4,320 = ₹28,320

The ₹4,320 is not yours. You pay it to the government.

Registration is compulsory above ₹20 lakh of yearly sales, as of November 2026. Whether you need it yet is on Service tax (GST): do you need it yet?

Tax she may hold back. A business client may keep part of your fee and pay it to the tax office in your name.

This is TDS (tax deducted at source). For professional work it is 10% of the fee, without GST.

Fee on the invoice           = ₹24,000
TDS at 10%                   = ₹24,000 × 0.10 = ₹2,400
Money that reaches you       = ₹24,000 − ₹2,400 = ₹21,600

The ₹2,400 is not lost. It counts as income tax you have already paid, and you see it in your yearly tax statement on the income tax website.

TDS applies only once her payments to you pass ₹50,000 in the year, as of November 2026. Asha's sprint is ₹48,000, so she may pay in full. If she adds a care plan later, TDS will start.

If ₹21,600 arrives, do not chase the ₹2,400. Ask her for the TDS certificate instead.

Numbers, due dates and how she pays

Numbering. Use one unbroken series for each financial year, which runs from 1 April to 31 March. For example: PK/26-27/001, then PK/26-27/002.

Never skip or reuse a number, even for a cancelled invoice. A gap makes a tax officer ask questions. On 1 April 2027, start again at PK/27-28/001.

The final invoice for Asha is the next number in the series, whatever it is. If you invoiced another client in between, that is fine.

Due date. Always write a date, not "on receipt". Your agreement says 7 days, so the invoice says the same.

How she can pay. Bank transfer or UPI is best for Indian clients: no fee for either side. A payment link from Razorpay is easier for her, but costs you about 2%.

When the money arrives. Mark the invoice as paid in your records the same day. Then send one line: "Received ₹24,000 for PK/26-27/001, thank you."

It closes the loop for both of you.

What changes for a foreign client

Say a made-up US client pays you $850 for an audit. The invoice changes in four ways:

  • The amount is in USD: $850.
  • There is no GST line, even if you are registered.
  • The tax note says: "Export of services. No GST charged."
  • The payment details are for your foreign-payment service, not your Indian bank.

Keep the bank's proof of each foreign payment. How that money reaches you is on Getting paid from abroad.

When to send each invoice

  1. Send the deposit invoice the day she says yes, with the agreement.
  2. Start work only when the deposit reaches your account.
  3. Send the final invoice the day she approves the work in writing.
  4. Send the handover pack once the final invoice is paid.

If a payment is late, keep it calm and regular. On the due date, send a short reminder:

Hi Asha, a gentle reminder that invoice PK/26-27/001 for ₹24,000 is due today. I have attached it again. Thank you!

Most late payments are forgotten, not refused. If a week passes with no reply, follow the steps on The client is not paying.
