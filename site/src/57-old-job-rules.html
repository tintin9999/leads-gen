Your warmest early leads are people from your past work. Before you pitch any of them, check two sets of promises.

One set is from your old job at Noovosoft. The other is with your Cultus co-founders.

This page is general information, not legal advice. If a clause in your papers worries you, show it to a lawyer before you act.

A tempting message

In December, you want to write to an engineering manager you worked with on the Deribit project. You plan to say: "I'm freelancing now. Do you need help with your admin tools?"

Deribit was a client of Noovosoft, your old employer. You worked on it as a Noovosoft employee. Your appointment letter may contain a promise not to approach Noovosoft's clients for some months.

Sending that message could break a promise you signed. It could also cost you a friendly link with Noovosoft. Ten minutes with your papers first is cheap.

Part one: read your Noovosoft papers

Find every paper you signed or received. Then read each one for three kinds of promise.

  1. Collect your offer letter, appointment letter, any secrecy agreement, and your exit papers (the relieving letter).
  2. Also look for any secrecy agreements you signed for the Deribit or Twinntax projects.
  3. Search each document for the words "confidential", "non-disclosure", "solicit", "non-compete" and "restraint".
  4. For each clause you find, note its type, how long it lasts, and from which date.
  5. Keep the notes in one file with the documents.

The three kinds of promise are these:

  • A secrecy promise (confidentiality clause): you will not share the employer's or its clients' private information.
  • A promise not to approach clients (non-solicitation clause): you will not pitch the employer's clients, or hire its staff, for a set time.
  • A promise not to compete (non-compete clause): you will not do similar work for a set time after you leave.

What Indian law says, in outline

Here is the general position as of November 2026.

  • A ban on competing after the job ends is generally not enforceable in India. Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act voids agreements that stop people from working. The Delhi High Court said this again strictly in 2025.
  • Secrecy still binds you. A confidentiality clause lasts after you leave, and courts enforce it.
  • Client-approach clauses are a grey area. Courts have decided both ways. It depends on the wording, the time limit and the facts.

So you can freelance in the same field, building React apps, without worry. The careful part is who you approach and what you share.

The safe default for Deribit and Twinntax

Until any client-approach period has ended, follow these rules:

  • Do not pitch people at Deribit or Twinntax for paid work.
  • You may stay friendly and keep in touch. Congratulate them, share articles, answer questions.
  • If one of them asks you for help first, that is different. Keep the message that shows they started it.
  • Never use code, documents, screenshots, internal names or numbers from Noovosoft, Deribit or Twinntax.

Describing the work in public is fine if you stay general. Say what you did, not how their systems work inside.

"Built back-office admin tools in React and TypeScript for Deribit, a crypto derivatives exchange owned by Coinbase. Moved old Angular screens to React and added the Vitest test tool to the build."

If a secrecy agreement forbids naming the client, use a description instead, such as "a large crypto derivatives exchange". Never add numbers that are not public.

Part two: agree the rules with your Cultus co-founders

You built Cultus with two co-founders, and you co-own it. Freelancing can clash with it in two ways: your time and its brand partners.

Cultus has 12 brand partners. Asha is a made-up owner of "Indigo Thread", a Pune clothing brand on Shopify. Say she sells on Cultus and asks you for a ₹48,000 Store Speed Sprint on her own store.

Is that fine with your co-founders? Do they expect you on Cultus 30 hours a week? Without a written answer, a friendly team can fall out.

Agree these points in writing, before you take paid work:

  1. How many hours a week you give Cultus.
  2. Whether you may offer paid services to Cultus brand partners, and on what terms.
  3. What is off-limits, for example work for a competing marketplace.
  4. That Cultus code stays with Cultus. You show it as proof, but never copy it into client work.

A short email, answered "agreed" by both co-founders, is enough:

"Hi both, I'm starting freelance work alongside Cultus. To keep things clear, I suggest this:

1. I give Cultus 15 hours a week.

2. I may offer paid services to Cultus brand partners, but I tell you first each time.

3. I won't work for a competing marketplace.

4. Cultus code stays with Cultus, and I won't reuse it for clients.

Please reply 'agreed' or suggest changes. We can review this in 6 months."

Save the replies in the same file as your Noovosoft notes.
