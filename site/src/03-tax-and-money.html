Bottom line. File under the presumptive scheme for professionals: s.58 of the Income-tax Act 2025, which replaced 44ADA from 1 April 2026. It treats 50% of gross receipts as your profit, with no expense accounting for tax, up to ₹75L if no more than 5% of receipts are in cash. Combine it with the new regime's ₹12L rebate and ₹24L gross ≈ ₹0 income tax, ₹36L ≈ ₹1.66L (4.6%) and ₹75L ≈ ₹7.33L (9.8%). Pay all advance tax in one instalment by 15 March. Collect foreign money through a licensed cross-border rail with a free FIRA, which costs about 1% all-in, instead of PayPal at about 8%. GST becomes mandatory near ₹20L. Register then and file the LUT so exports stay at 0%. Move 10% of every foreign receipt into a tax-reserve account until your first return shows you need less. Figures are as of Nov 2026. Presumptive taxation (s.58): your default

  • How it works: you declare 50% of gross receipts as profit, or more if your real profit is higher. All expenses (laptop, software, internet, coworking, depreciation) are treated as already covered. You don't itemise them for tax.
  • Who: resident individuals and non-LLP partnership firms in a specified profession. Individual software and IT services work has long been treated as eligible: "information technology" is a notified profession under the old 44AA/Rule 6F, and commentary says the 2025 Act's s.62(4) list keeps it. Have your CA pick the nature-of-business code on the first return.
  • Limit: gross receipts up to ₹50L, or ₹75L if cash receipts are 5% or less. Bank transfers, UPI, cards and foreign remittances all count as non-cash, so you qualify for ₹75L.
  • No lock-in for professionals. The five-year bar in s.58(7) applies to the business scheme, not to professionals. You can opt in or out year by year. If you go below 50%, you need full books and a tax audit.
  • Restrictions: s.58(4) limits other deductions and loss set-offs against presumptive income. You have no losses, so this rarely matters.
  • Don't file software development under the 6%/8% business scheme. Practitioners treat it as a profession, so the professional scheme at 50% is the safe one.
  • When to drop it: only if real expenses exceed 50% of receipts. That realistically happens when you resell subcontractors' work, which is also an entity-upgrade trigger.
  • Records anyway: keep invoices, the receipts log, the expense log and bank statements. ITR-4 asks for year-end debtors, creditors, bank and cash balances. Clean records end any argument about the books requirement.

Old regime: why it loses for you

Old-regime slabs are nil to ₹2.5L, 5% to ₹5L, 20% to ₹10L and 30% above, with a rebate only up to ₹5L. At ₹24L gross (₹12L deemed), even with the full ₹1.5L under 80C and ₹25k under 80D, taxable income is ₹10.25L and tax is about ₹1.25L, against ₹0 in the new regime. A professional who opts out of the new regime can switch back only once, so don't experiment. Stay in the new regime unless a CA shows otherwise, for example with a large home loan.

Advance tax: one date, 15 March

  • Rule: if you declare under s.58, pay 100% of the year's advance tax by 15 March (s.408(2)). This applies only if tax after TDS is ₹10,000 or more.
  • Late: a payment by 31 March still counts as advance tax but costs about 1% interest on the shortfall (s.425). If less than 90% is paid by 31 March, interest is 1% per month until you pay (s.424).
  • If you leave presumptive: the normal four instalments apply: 15% by 15 Jun, 45% by 15 Sep, 75% by 15 Dec, 100% by 15 Mar.
  • How: incometax.gov.in → e-Pay Tax → Advance tax, for tax year 2026-27. Save the challan in /Tax.
  • Routine: on 1 March, estimate the full year in the calculator, subtract TDS from Form 168/AIS, and pay from the tax reserve.

GST in practice

Do you need it?

See the decision tree in Business Setup. In short: ₹20L all-India aggregate turnover (by PAN, including exports, excluding GST) is the mandatory line. For export-only freelancers below ₹20L, the two readings of the law are:

ViewArgumentImplication
A (prevailing)Notification 10/2017-Integrated Tax exempts inter-state service suppliers below ₹20L from registration, and exports of services are inter-state supplies (s.7(5) IGST Act).No registration needed below ₹20L. Most freelancer guides and the payment platforms follow this.
B (conservative)Zero-rated supplies need registration under s.24 CGST to "claim" zero-rating and refunds. A CBIC FAQ notes that registration is needed to claim refunds.Register voluntarily if you want ITC refunds or certainty.

Export of services: the five conditions (s.2(6) IGST Act)

  1. You (the supplier) are in India.
  2. The recipient is outside India.
  3. The place of supply is outside India. For most IT services that is the recipient's location (s.13(2) IGST).
  4. Payment is received in convertible foreign exchange, or in INR where RBI permits. Payment through a cross-border aggregator that converts to INR still qualifies, and the FIRA proves it.
  5. You and the recipient are not merely branches of the same person.

Codes, LUT and invoice rules

  • SAC codes: 998314 IT design & development (software, web and app development, which is your default). 998313 IT consulting & support (audits, advisory, support-only retainers). 998315 hosting and IT infrastructure provisioning. 998319 other IT services. The rate is 18%, unchanged by the 2025 GST rationalisation.
  • LUT (Form GST RFD-11): gst.gov.in → Services → User Services → Furnish Letter of Undertaking. It is free and online, and the ARN is your LUT reference. It's valid for one financial year. File it before the first export invoice of each year, ideally in March for the next year.
  • Timing: issue a tax invoice within 30 days of completing the service (Rule 47).
  • Numbering: consecutive, unique per financial year, at most 16 characters, using letters, digits, "-" and "/" only. For example PK/26-27/001.
  • Export endorsement (Rule 46): "SUPPLY MEANT FOR EXPORT UNDER BOND OR LETTER OF UNDERTAKING WITHOUT PAYMENT OF INTEGRATED TAX". The full statutory text reads "SUPPLY MEANT FOR EXPORT/SUPPLY TO SEZ UNIT OR SEZ DEVELOPER FOR AUTHORISED OPERATIONS UNDER BOND OR LETTER OF UNDERTAKING WITHOUT PAYMENT OF INTEGRATED TAX", and using it verbatim is safest. Export invoices also show the recipient's name and address, the country of destination and the currency, plus the INR value at the invoice-date exchange rate (Rule 34; follow your CA's convention).
  • The full field list is in invoice checklist, and the Invoice Generator produces all three formats.

Returns and credits (once registered)

  • QRMP (turnover up to ₹5 Cr): GSTR-1 quarterly by the 13th of the month after the quarter. GSTR-3B quarterly by the 22nd (Maharashtra's group; confirm on the portal). Pay PMT-06 by the 25th for months 1–2 of a quarter if any tax is due. Nil returns are still mandatory and late fees apply. Annual GSTR-9 is optional below ₹2 Cr.
  • Input tax credit: GST paid on business purchases (laptop, monitor, coworking, Indian SaaS) can offset output GST. Exporters under LUT accumulate ITC and can claim a refund (RFD-01, s.54(3)). That's worth it for big purchases, such as ₹36k back on a ₹2L laptop.
  • Platforms: Upwork's service fee is variable (0–15% per contract), and India-based freelancers pay 18% GST on that fee. That is a cost to you, not GST you collect. A 10% fee therefore costs about 11.8% of the contract value.
  • Imported services: once registered, foreign SaaS bought for the business may trigger reverse-charge GST. Ask your CA how to handle it.

Foreign clients: FIRA, purpose code, realisation, EDF, W-8BEN

FIRA (get one per payment)

The Foreign Inward Remittance Advice proves the money came from abroad as payment for a service export. You need it as GST zero-rating proof, for bank and EDF closure, and to support your ITR. Skydo, BriskPe and Razorpay issue e-FIRAs free. Banks issue them on request, sometimes for a fee. PayPal issues monthly ones. "FIRC" is the older name. An e-BRC (DGFT) is for goods and incentive claims, and freelancers rarely need one.

Purpose code

Default to P0802 (software consultancy and implementation), which is the common code for freelance development and consulting routed through aggregators. Banks sometimes use P0807 (off-site software exports) for purely remote development. What matters is consistency between the invoice description, purpose code and books. Never let a payment be tagged as family maintenance or a gift.

Realisation period

The FEMA (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations 2026 took effect on 1 Oct 2026. As amended on 22 Sept 2026, they require service-export proceeds to be realised within 9 months of the invoice date (12 months if invoiced or settled in INR). Net-7 terms make this a non-issue, but chase anything older than 60 days.

EDF (new from 1 Oct 2026)

Service exporters now declare exports on an Export Declaration Form, which replaced SOFTEX. RBI's 2 Nov 2026 amendment, retrospective to 1 Oct, treats resident individuals (freelancers, consultants) as having filed the EDF once they give their bank the inward-remittance details. Firms and companies get the same treatment for remittances up to ₹10L. Action: answer your bank's or aggregator's invoice and purpose-code requests promptly. If you incorporate later, EDF filing applies to you in full.

W-8BEN for US clients

  • Why: it certifies that you are not a US person. Without it, US payers may ask for a W-9 or withhold 24% backup withholding. Services you perform physically in India are generally foreign-source income, so no US withholding should apply.
  • How: Part I: name; citizenship India; permanent address in India; US TIN left blank; foreign TIN = your PAN; date of birth. Part II (treaty claim) is optional for work done outside the US. Many Indian freelancers cite Article 15 (Independent Personal Services) of the India–US treaty at 0%. Then sign and date it.
  • Validity: until 31 December of the third calendar year after signing (signed Nov 2026, valid to 31 Dec 2029).
  • Never sign a W-9, which is for US persons. Platforms (Upwork, Contra) collect the W-8BEN information in their tax settings.
  • If US tax was withheld anyway: get it fixed at source first. Otherwise claim a foreign tax credit in India on Form 44 (ex-Form 67), filed before the ITR due date, or file a US refund claim. Both are slow, so prevention is the real fix.
  • Other countries: UK and EU clients rarely withhold on services. Some Asian payers do. Use the treaty rate with an Indian Tax Residency Certificate. Ask your CA before signing such a contract.

Money routine: buckets, reserves, monthly close

Five buckets

  1. Operating (inflow) account: every receipt lands here. Business expenses leave from here.
  2. Tax reserve: move the reserve % the day money lands (table below). Don't touch it until 1 March, or until refunds arrive.
  3. GST account (once registered): move 100% of GST collected straight away. It's the government's money.
  4. Runway: 6 months of personal expenses + 3 months of business costs. Refill it before you increase your own pay.
  5. Personal: a fixed "salary" transfer on the 1st. A good rule is 70% of the trailing 3-month average of net receipts after reserves. The rest builds runway.

Ten expensive mistakes to avoid

  1. Defaulting to PayPal: about 8% of every foreign invoice gone.
  2. Receiving client money into a personal account mixed with family transfers.
  3. The wrong purpose code (gift or family maintenance) on export income.
  4. Missing 15 March advance tax, then scrambling.
  5. Not checking AIS / Form 168. A mismatch triggers automated notices.
  6. Charging "GST" without a GSTIN, or registering and forgetting the LUT, which turns exports into 18% IGST.
  7. Late nil GST returns. The late fees add up quietly.
  8. Spending TDS money as if it were income. It's a refund you will get, not cash you have.
  9. Mixing Cultus money with freelance money.
  10. Not filing the FY 2025-26 return before the 31 Dec 2026 belated deadline.
