Clients often ask for "one more small thing" in the middle of a job. This page gives you one rule for that moment. Every new ask gets a price and a date before you start it.

"While you're in there…"

Maya is a made-up founder in the US. Her app "Fernleaf" was built with an AI app builder on Next.js and Supabase. She hired you on Upwork for a $300 fixed-price job: fix the login bug and the Stripe payment bug.

On day 2, the login fix is done. Then she writes:

"Great work! While you're in there, can you also add Google login? Should be quick."

Google login was not in the job. It needs a Google project, new Supabase settings, a new button, and tests on two browsers. That is about 3 hours of work.

If you say "sure" and do it free, you lose 3 hours. Worse, the Stripe fix now slips, and the next ask feels free too.

The fix is simple. Do not say yes or no on the spot. Say you will size it, then give her a price and a date.

The reply to send

Reply warmly the same day. Keep the current job safe, and offer choices.

"Good idea. Google login is outside this job, so I want to keep the Stripe fix on track first.

Adding Google login is about 3 hours. I can do it for $120, delivered 2 days after the Stripe fix. Or we can leave it for a later job.

If you want it now, please add it as a new milestone and fund it. I'll start once it shows as funded."

You gave her two choices, not a "no". She decides the trade-off.

The new work comes after the work she already paid for. Never let a new ask push back an agreed delivery without her saying yes in writing.

A simple test: is this inside the job?

Open the contract description and your plan in the Upwork chat. Then ask three questions about the new request.

  1. Is it named in the contract description or the agreed plan? If yes, it is inside the job.
  2. Is it needed to make a named item work? For example, a test for the Stripe fix is inside the job.
  3. Is it new behaviour the user can see, or a new screen, page or service? If yes, it is new work.

Google login fails question 1 and passes question 3. It is new work. A typo you notice in the login screen you are already fixing is inside the job.

If you are unsure, treat it as new work and ask.

Add the work properly on Upwork

A fixed-price job on Upwork is paid in steps, each with its own payment (milestones). The client puts the money for each step with Upwork before it starts. Upwork holds it until she approves your work.

For extra work on a fixed-price job, ask for a new milestone:

  1. Agree the extra work, the price and the date in the Upwork chat.
  2. Ask the client to open the contract and add a new milestone with that amount.
  3. Wait until the new milestone shows as funded on the contract page.
  4. Do the work, then submit it for that milestone.

A bonus is a payment the client sends on top, with no milestone. It suits a tiny extra she offers to pay for after it is done. It is not protected in advance, so use it only for small amounts.

On an hourly job, extra work is simpler. You log the hours with the Upwork desktop app, and she pays for them each week. Still tell her first:

"Google login is outside what we planned. It is about 3 hours on this contract. OK to go ahead after the Stripe fix?"

Wait for her "yes" in the chat. That message is your record if she later questions the hours.

With a direct client

A direct client is one you found outside a portal. There is no Upwork to hold money, so a short written note does the job (change note).

Asha is a made-up owner of "Indigo Thread", a Pune clothing brand on Shopify. She bought your ₹48,000 Store Speed Sprint. Mid-way, she asks you to also speed up her blog pages.

"Hi Asha, good idea. The blog pages are outside the Sprint, so here are three options.

A) Add them now: ₹9,000, delivery moves from 20 to 23 November. B) Swap them for the size-chart page, no change in price or date. C) Do them in a later round.

I'd go with C, so the Sprint finishes on time. Reply 'Approved A', 'B' or 'C' and I'll start."

Send it by email, so the record lasts. Add the approved amount to the next invoice, and write the new total and date in your reply.

The full email version is in the change request template. Quick answers for other moments are in the extra-work replies.

Small favours you choose to give

Some asks really are tiny: a button colour, a changed word. You may choose to do these free. Keep it to about one hour a week in total.

Always say it was free, so it does not become the rule:

"Quick one, so I've done it at no charge. Anything bigger I'll price first, so the delivery date stays safe."

Do not do anything free that touches login, payments or customer data. Those need proper tests, so they always get a price.

If three or more new asks arrive in one week, pause and agree priorities with the client. Read I am stuck or will miss the deadline if the date is already at risk.

Do this now

  • Save the "Google login" reply as a note you can copy and edit.
  • Write the three-question test on the paper by your desk.
  • Read the change request template once.
  • On your next job, keep a list of every free favour you give.
