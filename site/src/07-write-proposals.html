A good proposal is short and is about the client's problem, not about you. This page shows one finished proposal, then takes it apart line by line. It also says what reply rate is normal, so silence does not scare you.

The finished proposal

Maya is our made-up client, a US founder. Her post: "Fix login and Stripe checkout bugs in my Next.js + Supabase app", fixed price, $300.

Her post says users get logged out right after they sign in. It also says some paid orders stay marked "pending".

Your two bugs sound like a lost login session and a Stripe payment message your app never receives. Both are common in apps made with AI builders, and both can be fixed this week.

I built Cultus (cultus.co.in), a live Next.js and Supabase marketplace with 180+ paid orders and 115 test files on its payment code.

My plan:

  1. Day 1: repeat both bugs on a test copy and send you a short video.
  2. Days 2 and 3: fix login, then checkout, with a test for each.
  3. Day 4: you try it, then I hand over notes in plain English.

One question: do "pending" orders happen with every card, or only some?

$300 fixed, 4 days. I attached a 2-minute video of where I would start.

That is about 120 words. Maya can read it in 30 seconds on her phone.

Why each line is there

  • The first two lines. In her list of proposals, Maya sees only the start of each one. So your first lines name her problem and your guess at the cause.
  • Proof in one line. One real project that matches her stack, with two numbers. Do not list your whole history: your profile does that.
  • A small plan. Three steps with days. It shows you have done this before, and it tells her when she will hear from you.
  • One question. An easy question about her bug gives her a reason to reply. Ask only one.
  • Price and time. Say both plainly. If her budget is fair, use her number.

Never start with "Hi, I am a developer with 4 years of experience". Fifty other proposals start that way, and she will skip them.

Do not put your email, phone number or a calendar link in a proposal. Sharing contact details before a contract breaks Upwork's rules and can cost you the account.

Her extra questions and the video

Some posts add questions you must answer when you apply (screening questions). Freelancers report that the client sees these answers before your cover letter. So write them with the same care.

Question: "Describe your recent experience with similar projects."

On Cultus I built login with one-time codes and the full payment flow on Next.js and Supabase. I wrote tests for every money path, so a paid order can never stay "pending". I can show you how that code is laid out on a call.

Keep each answer to two or three sentences, with one real fact in it. More ready answers are in the proposal kit.

A short screen recording helps a new profile a lot, because few people make one. Record 2 minutes with Loom or a similar tool.

  1. Open her public app, or her post if the app is private.
  2. Say her name and her problem in the first 10 seconds.
  3. Show where you would look first, and why.
  4. Stop at 2 minutes. Do not show your email or phone number on screen.
  5. Attach the file, or paste the link in the last line of the proposal.

Make a video only for jobs that score 7 or 8 on the 2-minute check. Never log in to her app or run her code at this stage.

Should you pay to boost?

You apply with paid tokens called Connects, at $0.15 each. A boost is extra Connects you bid to put your proposal in the top four places of her list.

It works like an auction, as of November 2026. You set the highest number of Connects you will pay. The four highest bids get the top places.

You pay the bid if Maya opens, shortlists, answers or declines your boosted proposal. You also pay if you are still in the top four when the auction ends.

The auction ends after seven days, or when she hires. If you are pushed out before she touches your proposal, you get the bid back.

A boost buys a place in the list. It does not buy a reply. So use this plain rule:

  • Boost only a job that scores 8 out of 8 and is under two hours old.
  • Bid 15 Connects at most, which is $2.25 or about ₹214 at ₹95 to $1.
  • Boost two proposals a week at most. If top four costs more than 15, do not boost.

How many to send, and what is normal

Send two or three careful proposals a day, five days a week. Ten fast ones do worse than three careful ones, and cost more.

Proposals in 4 weeks:   12 a week × 4 weeks = 48
Replies at 5% to 10%:   48 × 0.05 = 2    up to   48 × 0.10 = 5
Jobs at 1 in 20 to 40:  48 ÷ 40 = 1      up to   48 ÷ 20 = 2

These rates come from tool sellers and freelancer reports, not from Upwork. Treat them as rough.

So in your first 20 proposals, one or two replies is normal. Zero replies happens too, and does not mean you are bad.

If you reach 20 with no reply at all, open I sent 20 proposals and got no replies.
