This page puts steps 1 to 9 on a calendar. It shows what to do each week, what it costs, and which five numbers to check on Fridays. Use it as a map, not as an exam.

A normal week, in numbers

It is the end of your third week. You write down five numbers.

Proposals sent this week:   12
Replies from clients:        1
Calls held:                  1
Jobs won:                    0
Money earned:               $0

Reply rate: 1 reply ÷ 12 proposals = 8%

This looks bad, and it is a normal week. A new profile gets a reply to about 5 to 10 of every 100 proposals.

So one reply from 12 proposals is on track. The first job usually comes from the second or third reply.

Keep this example in mind on slow weeks. Silence is part of the start, and it is not a verdict on you.

Each row has one finish line. When you reach it, move to the next row, even if the week is not over.

If a row takes longer, do not skip ahead. A weak profile in week 1 makes every later week harder.

Your dates may move by a week or two. That is fine. The order matters more than the speed.

If your profile is not approved in week 1, open Upwork rejected my profile or paused my account.

Your working day

From week 2, every working day has the same small shape. It takes about two hours.

  1. Morning: check your saved searches for 20 minutes.
  2. Pick two or three posts that pass the check from step 6.
  3. Write one careful proposal for each. Allow about 20 minutes a proposal.
  4. Afternoon: check the searches again for 20 minutes.
  5. Late evening: check once more. Clients in the US post during their morning.
  6. Answer any client message within a few hours.
  7. Send one backup message, as in step 11.

Keep the two hours at the same time each day. A fixed slot is easier to keep than a floating one.

When you have a paid job, the job comes first. Still send at least one proposal a day, so the next job is on its way.

What it costs

Applying on Upwork costs tokens called Connects. You buy them in small packs.

Proposals a week:        about 12
Connects a week:         about 180
Cost a week:             180 × $0.15       = $27
In rupees, at ₹95 to $1: $27 × 95          = ₹2,565
Weeks of applying:       weeks 2 to 8      = 7 weeks
Whole of Stage 1:        ₹2,565 × 7        = ₹17,955

Tax may be added when you pay, and the live exchange rate will differ a little. Plan for about ₹20,000 in total.

Think of it as the price of a short course. One $300 job pays most of it back.

Buy Connects one week at a time. If a week goes badly, you fix the cause before you spend more.

The Friday check

Every Friday, spend 20 minutes on the same five numbers. Write them in a note or in the pipeline tracker.

  • Proposals sent. The target is 10 to 15 a week.
  • Replies. About one reply for every 10 to 20 proposals is normal at first.
  • Calls held. Most replies should turn into a short call or chat.
  • Jobs won. One job from every two or three calls is a good sign.
  • Money earned. Count only money that has reached Upwork, not promises.

Then look for one of these three patterns. Each has its own page.

When to raise your rate

Your rate moves up with your reviews. It does not move with the calendar.

  • First three jobs: small fixed-price jobs of $150 to $500, or $35 to $40 an hour.
  • After three good reviews: $45 to $50 an hour.
  • After five or more reviews and a strong score: $55 to $70 an hour.

Never go below $35 an hour. A very low price brings the hardest clients and the weakest reviews.

Five good reviews also mark the end of Stage 1. Then read Work out your minimum rate and start Stage 2.
