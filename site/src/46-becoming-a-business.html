In law, you are already a business. You do not need to register anything to start. The paperwork comes later, in small steps, and each step has a clear trigger.

The day the first payment arrives

Maya, a made-up founder in the US, pays $300 for your login fix on Upwork. The money reaches your bank a week later.

On that day you are the sole owner of a one-person business. This is called a sole proprietor. You filed nothing and paid nothing to become one.

A sole proprietor is not separate from you. Your PAN is the business's tax number. Your profit goes in your own income tax return.

It also means you are personally responsible for the business. Your shield is a clear contract, and on Upwork, Upwork's own terms.

Everything below is optional until its trigger arrives. Most of it you can ignore for months.

Now: a separate bank account

Trigger: now. Cost: ₹0. Time: 30 minutes.

Pick one savings account only for work. Every client payment lands there, and every work cost leaves from there. Your personal spending happens elsewhere.

Keep it apart from Cultus money too. Freelance clients and Cultus should never share an account. Create a UPI ID on the work account for small Indian payments.

This makes your income easy to prove. It also keeps your chartered accountant's work small and cheap. A second account for your tax savings helps too, as shown in A 30-minute money routine each month.

First Indian business client: the free small-business registration

Trigger: before you sign your first contract with an Indian company. Cost: ₹0. Time: 20–30 minutes.

The government's free registration for small businesses is called Udyam. You sign up on udyamregistration.gov.in with your Aadhaar and PAN. You get a certificate with a permanent number.

It gives you a legal right to be paid on time by Indian business clients. They must pay within 45 days. After that, they owe interest at three times the bank rate set by India's central bank.

Asha, who runs the made-up Pune brand Indigo Thread, pays your ₹60,000 invoice one month after the 45 days.

Bank rate, as of November 2026             5.75% a year
Late-payment interest:  3 × 5.75%        = 17.25% a year
Per month:  17.25% ÷ 12                  ≈ 1.44%
Interest for one month late:
  ₹60,000 × 1.44%                        ≈ ₹864

You will rarely need to claim it. Putting the line on your invoice is usually enough to get paid first.

Use only the .gov.in site. Lookalike sites charge ₹1,000–3,000 to "file" it, and some collect Aadhaar data.

Register before signing that contract. Courts disagree on whether the right covers contracts signed before registration.

Maharashtra professional tax: ₹2,500 a year

Trigger: you work for yourself in Maharashtra. Cost: ₹2,500 a year. Time: about 30 minutes on mahagst.gov.in.

Maharashtra charges self-employed professionals a small flat yearly tax. You enrol once, then pay each June. Some updates say the date moved to 15 June, so check on the site.

The certificate also helps later, as proof of your business when you open a current account.

Later steps, each with its trigger

  • Service tax registration (GST): when your yearly receipts reach about ₹15 lakh, before the ₹20 lakh line. Registering is free, but the returns take about an hour a month. See Service tax (GST): do you need it yet?
  • A business name: only if you plan to build a small team. Your own name sells best for now. Using a name is free; a trademark costs ₹4,500 per class, as of November 2026.
  • A current account: a bank account in the business's name, in Stage 3. The bank asks for two business proofs, such as Udyam and the professional tax certificate. It usually needs an average balance of ₹10,000–25,000.
  • A limited company or a partnership: only when receipts near ₹60–75 lakh a year, when you hire, or when a partner joins.

A partnership with limited liability (LLP) costs about ₹7,000–15,000 to set up and ₹10,000–40,000 a year to run. A private limited company costs about ₹10,000–20,000 to set up and ₹25,000–1.5 lakh a year.

Tax is not a reason to form a company below ₹75 lakh. As a sole proprietor using the simple scheme, you pay ₹0 on ₹24 lakh of receipts. Ask a chartered accountant about this one when you reach that level.

Do this now

  • Choose one savings account for work only, and move all client payments there.
  • Open a second account for your tax savings.
  • Enrol for professional tax on mahagst.gov.in and save the payment receipt.
  • Set a note: "Register on Udyam before the first Indian contract."
