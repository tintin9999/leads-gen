There are dozens of freelance sites, and each wants a profile. You need one main portal and one net. This page names the net, says why, and puts every other portal in a clear order.

One main portal, one net

It is week 2. Your Upwork profile is live, and you send 2 to 3 proposals a day.

On Saturday morning you spend 90 minutes on one more site, Arc.dev. You fill in your profile with Cultus and your work for a crypto exchange's back office. You book its technical check for the following week.

Then you close the tab and go back to Upwork. You do not open a third site.

Upwork stays your main portal for the first 8 weeks. That is where your first reviews come from.

A second portal is a net, not a second job. It should take one session to set up and almost no time each week.

A third, fourth and fifth profile would each need care to work. Spread that thin, and none of them works.

Why Arc.dev is the net

Arc.dev is a vetted network: a site that tests developers before showing them to clients. Clients come to it already wanting a tested developer.

  • It accepts India. Arc runs pages for India-based developers and lists many of them.
  • It is free for you. You pay no fee and no tokens to apply, as of November 2026.
  • It fits your stack. Its jobs are often longer React and Next.js contracts, which suit four years of experience.
  • Its rates fit your plan. Arc's own data shows a React median of $61 to $80 an hour, across all countries. Your share as an India-based developer may be lower.
  • It needs little upkeep. Once you pass the check, matches come to you. You do not write daily proposals.

The check takes time, often a week or more. That is why you start it in week 2, not after Upwork stalls.

  1. Sign up on Arc.dev as a developer, with your real full name.
  2. Paste your Upwork title and the first two paragraphs of your overview.
  3. Add Cultus and Bro as projects, with the same numbers as on Upwork.
  4. Set your rate at $40 an hour and choose "freelance" or "contract" work.
  5. Book the technical check for a day when you have two quiet hours.

Contra was the other choice. It takes no cut from you, but clients there mostly browse portfolios. With no reviews yet, Arc's test does more for you than a showcase.

What to do at each stage

Now (weeks 1 to 8). Upwork every day. Arc.dev once, in week 2, then check its emails twice a week.

After 3 Upwork reviews. Add a free Contra profile, reusing your Upwork text and portfolio. Apply to Flexiple and Braintrust, which also test you first.

By then you have reviews to show. Each new site then takes one session, because the text already exists.

Later (Stage 2, after about 5 reviews). Toptal, but only after 10 to 14 days of practice. Its test includes timed coding problems and a live build.

Its checks take two weeks or more. A rushed attempt wastes those weeks, so do not try it unprepared.

Some "portals" ask you to clone and run a repo as a test. That is a known trick to steal keys. Read A stranger asks you to run their code first.

What you can skip

You do not need these yet, and some you never need.

  • Fiverr. It takes 20% of every order, and web jobs there are heavily undercut.
  • Freelancer.com, PeoplePerHour, Guru, Truelancer. Mostly low-budget bidding. Skip them for the first 90 days.
  • Gun.io and Lemon.io. They lean toward developers in the US, Canada or Europe.
  • Paid upgrades anywhere. Do not pay for a "Pro" plan until a free profile gets views.

The Shopify and Razorpay partner programmes are not portals. They give tools and some trust for Indian brand work. Selling to Indian brands on Shopify covers them.
