Bottom line.
  • Floors: ₹1,200/hr in India, $35/hr globally. Never go below them. Cut scope instead.
  • Quote fixed prices for packaged outcomes. Build them internally from ₹1,500–2,500/hr and $40–60/hr, add a 20–40% risk buffer, then sanity-check the price against the value to the client.
  • Always give three options (Essential / Recommended / Premium). Take 50% upfront.
  • Re-price after 3 case studies and 3 testimonials. Then raise 10–20% every three wins or whenever you're booked more than six weeks out.
  • Global work pays 2–3× more per hour. ₹95 = $1, so $50/hr ≈ ₹4,750/hr. Track A gets you started; move hours to Track B as soon as it converts.

Why salary ÷ 160 is the wrong maths

As an employee you were paid for every working hour, including holidays, training, meetings and quiet weeks. As a business you are only paid for billable hours. You also pay for everything your employer used to cover.

Unpaid time

Selling, proposals, admin, invoicing, learning and gaps between projects. Year-one utilization of 50–60% is normal: 20–24 billable hours in a 40-hour week.

Costs you now carry

Health insurance, laptop, AI and software subscriptions, internet, a CA, coworking, no PF contribution, no paid leave.

Fees and friction

Platform fees (Upwork 0–15% + 18% GST on the fee), FX conversion (0.5–3%), late payers, TDS held back until you file.

One thing that helps

Presumptive taxation: with receipts up to ~₹24L, income tax is usually zero (50% deemed income, then the ₹12L rebate). Details in Tax & Money.

Building a fixed price

Price = round_up( Σ PERT estimates × internal rate × (1 + risk buffer) × (1 + PM overhead) )
PERT estimate = (optimistic + 4 × likely + pessimistic) ÷ 6
Risk buffer   = 20% familiar · 30% some unknowns · 40%+ legacy code / third-party APIs
PM overhead   = 10–15% (calls, updates, reviews, deploys, handover)

A1 Store Speed Sprint (India)

Audit 4h · app/script diet 5h · images/fonts 5h · theme fixes 7h · report and handover 3h = 24h

24 × ₹1,800 = ₹43,200 · +25% risk = ₹54,000 · +10% PM = ₹59,400

Quote: ₹48k Recommended, ₹30k Essential, ₹75k Premium

B1 audit + sprint (global)

Audit: 14h × $50 = $700 · +20% = $840 → quote $850

Sprint: 60h × $50 = $3,000 · +25% = $3,750 · +10% = $4,125

Quote: $3,200 / $5,400 / $8,500 (three tiers, below)

Track your actuals. Log hours privately even on fixed-price work (Toggl or Clockify). After each project compute the effective hourly rate = fee ÷ hours actually spent. If it drops below your MVR, your estimates or your scope control are broken. Fix them before the next quote.

Value pricing for D2C clients

Before quoting, estimate what the problem costs the client. If your price is a small fraction of the first year's value, the decision becomes easy. Ask for their numbers on the call (orders/month, AOV, COD share, RTO rate, conversion rate).

Example 2 — Store speed (A1)

A brand doing ₹25L/month online revenue. A Deloitte study commissioned by Google (2020) found that a 0.1 s improvement across key mobile speed metrics was associated with an 8.4% lift in retail conversions. Treat that as directional, not a promise. Even a conservative 2–3% conversion lift is worth ₹50,000–75,000 a month, so a ₹48,000 sprint pays for itself in under a month.

Use their own numbers on the call: "If conversion went from 1.8% to 1.9% on your traffic, what would that be worth per month?"

Example 3 — Internal tools (B2)

An ops team of 6 spends 1.5 hours a day each on manual approvals and reconciliation in spreadsheets: ~200 hours a month. At a fully loaded $15–30/hour (India/SEA ops) that's $3,000–6,000 a month, before counting errors. An $8,000 tool that removes half the work pays back in 3–5 months.

Rule of thumb. Price at roughly 10–25% of the first-year value you can credibly create, bounded below by your cost-based price. If you can't find meaningful value, either the client is the wrong fit or you're solving the wrong problem.

Three options: Good / Better / Best

A single price invites a yes/no decision and haggling. Three options turn the question into which, anchor the middle option, and let bigger buyers self-select into more scope. Typical ratio: 1 : 1.6 : 2.5. Mark the middle one "Recommended".

Payment terms

  • Under ₹2L / $5k: 50% to start, 50% on delivery (before handover of credentials and code ownership).
  • Above: 40% / 40% / 20% at kickoff, mid-milestone and acceptance.
  • Retainers: billed monthly in advance; unused hours roll over at most one month.
  • Weekly-rate work: invoiced weekly or fortnightly in advance.
  • Due date: Net 7. Late fee 1.5% per month (Indian B2B clients: you can cite statutory MSMED interest if Udyam-registered — see Contracts).
  • Kill fee: if the client cancels, they pay for work done plus 25% of the remaining milestone. The deposit is not refundable once work has started.
  • Transfer fees: the client pays their side; for SWIFT ask for "OUR" charges, or price in ~2% for fees.

Discount rules (and the "first clients" problem)

Never discount without getting something back. Trade price for:

  • Less scope (move to the Essential tier).
  • More time (a slower timeline that fits around other work).
  • Better cash flow (100% upfront → 5% off).
  • Proof: written testimonial, named case study, logo use, a 15-minute reference call.
  • Commitment: a three-month care plan after delivery.
Founding-client pricing (first three clients only). Instead of working cheaply forever, offer 25–30% off the standard price, in writing, to your first three clients in exchange for a case study, a testimonial and a referral call. Put an expiry on it ("founding rate valid until 15 Jan"). Never work for free: free work attracts the wrong clients and isn't valued.

Script — "Can you do it for less?"

"I can't lower the price for the same scope, but I can make it fit. If we drop the cart/checkout work and the 60-day care, the Essential option is ₹30,000 and still gets your product pages fast before the sale. Would that work?"

Answering "What's your rate?"

"Most of my work is fixed-price, so you know the total upfront. For example, my Store Speed Sprint is usually ₹30–75k depending on scope, and audits for MVPs start at $850. Can I ask a few questions about what you need? Then I'll send three options with exact prices within 24 hours."

If they insist on an hourly figure: give your standard rate (₹2,000/hr or $50/hr), not your floor, and steer back to scope. On marketplaces, see the rate ladder in market rates.

Ask about budget early (wording in Sales): "Projects like this usually land between ₹40k and ₹1.2L depending on scope. Where would you want this to sit?"
