Upwork feels scary until you see where the money goes. This page follows one $300 job from the job post to your bank. Every fee and every wait is shown with its number.

One job, from start to finish

Maya is a made-up founder in the US. Her app, Fernleaf, was built with an AI app builder on Next.js and Supabase.

She posts this job: "Fix login and Stripe checkout bugs in my Next.js + Supabase app". The price is fixed at $300.

The job post shows you two numbers: 12 Connects to apply, and a 10% Upwork fee.

Every job post shows these two numbers before you apply. They change from job to job, so read them each time.

What happens, in order

  1. You apply. Applying costs tokens called Connects. One Connect costs $0.15, and this job asks for 12.
  2. Maya reads your proposal and replies in Upwork Messages. You agree which bugs you will fix, and by which date.
  3. Maya sends an offer for $300. You accept it. Now there is a contract, and Upwork wrote it for you.
  4. Maya pays the $300 to Upwork before you start. Upwork holds it safely until the work is approved. This holding is called escrow.
  5. You fix the bugs. Then you submit the work for payment on the contract page.
  6. Maya approves. If she does nothing for 14 days, Upwork releases the money to you by itself.
  7. Upwork takes its fee and keeps the rest "Pending" for 5 days. This is the security wait.
  8. After 5 days the money is "Available". You withdraw it to your Indian bank. It arrives within 4 working days.

Do not start work until the contract page shows the $300 as funded. Work done before that has no protection.

The calculation, line by line

Before the job: applying
12 Connects × $0.15                      =   $1.80
Tax on Connects: $1.80 × 18%             =   $0.32
Cost to apply                            =   $2.12

The job
Maya pays                                = $300.00
Upwork fee: $300 × 10%                   = − $30.00
Tax on the fee: $30 × 18%                = −  $5.40
Income tax held back: $300 × 0.1%        = −  $0.30
Available after the 5-day wait           = $264.30
Withdrawal fee to an Indian bank         = −  $0.99
Sent to your bank                        = $263.31

In rupees, at ₹95 to $1
$263.31 × 95                             = ₹25,014
Cost to apply: $2.12 × 95                = −  ₹201
What you keep from this job              = ₹24,813

So on a job with a 10% fee, about 88% of the price becomes available to you. Remember that one number.

The fee is not always 10%. Upwork sets it per contract, from 0% to 15%, as of November 2026. The job post shows it, and it stays the same for that contract.

The 18% is India's Goods and Services Tax (GST). Upwork adds it to what it charges you: the fee and the Connects. It stops only if you give Upwork a GST number, and you do not have one yet.

The 0.1% is Indian income tax that Upwork holds back, called tax deducted at source (TDS). It needs your PAN, linked to Aadhaar, on your account. Without that, the rate is 5%.

TDS is not lost. It counts as tax you have already paid when you file your return.

Upwork converts dollars to rupees at its own rate. The withdrawal screen shows the amount before you confirm. It may be a little below the rate you see on Google.

Upwork publishes no fixed number of Connects per job. Other guides report about 10 to 20 for many jobs. Some new accounts get free Connects, but this is not promised, so plan to buy them.

Fixed-price jobs and hourly jobs

Maya's job is fixed-price: one agreed price for one agreed result. Your protection is the money she paid in before you started.

A bigger fixed-price job is split into parts called milestones. Each part is paid in before you start it, and each has its own 5-day wait.

An hourly job pays for your time. You run Upwork's desktop time tracker while you work. It records your hours and takes screenshots of your screen.

Upwork bills the client each week, from Monday to Sunday. Your money becomes available 10 days after that week ends.

Tracked hours are protected if the client does not pay. Hours you type in by hand are not. So always use the tracker on hourly jobs.

Reviews and the Job Success Score

When the contract ends, Maya rates you from 1 to 5 stars and can write a comment. You rate her too. Her stars and comment appear on your profile.

Upwork also shows a percentage on your profile, called the Job Success Score. It says how many of your contracts ended well.

In outline, Upwork takes good outcomes minus bad outcomes, and divides by all outcomes. Larger jobs count more.

A new freelancer has no score, and that is normal. Upwork's help page says it appears after two finished contracts with two different clients.

That is why your first three small jobs matter so much. Do the job and earn a five-star review shows how to finish them well.
