Sooner or later a client asks to "just do this outside Upwork". The answer is simple: before a contract, never. After, only through Upwork's own exit route, and only when the numbers make sense.

The message and your reply

Maya is our made-up client, a US founder. Her app "Fernleaf" was built with an AI app builder on Next.js and Supabase.

You have not started a contract yet. She writes: "can we just do this over WhatsApp and PayPal? Upwork fees are crazy."

You answer within the hour.

I understand, the fees add up. I keep all my work on Upwork, so we both have payment protection and a written record.

If you send the offer here, I can start tomorrow on the login fix.

Most clients say "fine" and send the offer. They were testing, not insisting.

If she still refuses, let the job go. A client who wants to skip the rules on day one often skips paying later.

What the rule says

Going around Upwork is called circumvention. It covers asking for, or agreeing to, payment outside Upwork with a client you met there.

It also covers suggesting it. Upwork says the breach counts even if no money ever moves outside.

As of November 2026, the rule lasts 24 months from the start of your work together on Upwork. During that time, all payments with that client must go through Upwork.

The punishment is the loss of your account, for both of you. Your reviews and earnings history go with it.

If a client asks for outside payment, decline. Upwork asks you to report the request. Use Report in the chat's menu.

Sharing an email after a contract starts is allowed, for example for GitHub access. Taking payment outside is not.

Upwork's own exit route

There is one legal way out. The client pays Upwork a one-time charge to move the relationship off the site (conversion fee).

As of November 2026, Upwork's terms work it out like this.

  1. Take your highest hourly rate with this client. On fixed-price work only, take your profile rate.
  2. Multiply it by 2,080 hours, which is one working year.
  3. Take 13.5% of that number.
  4. Subtract the fees the client paid Upwork on your work in the last 12 months.
  5. The result is at least $1,000 and at most $50,000.

After 24 months of working together on Upwork, the fee drops to $1. The client starts the request, and Upwork shows the exact amount.

Upwork also has Direct Contracts: you bring your own client and use Upwork's tools. They are only for clients who never had an Upwork account. So they cannot help with Maya.

When paying the fee is worth it

Six months in, Maya pays you $2,000 a month. Your profile rate is $40 an hour, and your Upwork fee is 10%. She asks about leaving.

Upwork adds India's Goods and Services Tax (GST) of 18% to its fee. The calculation below includes it.

The conversion fee (Maya pays it)
$40 × 2,080 hours                         =  $83,200
$83,200 × 13.5%                           =  $11,232
Minus her Upwork fees on you, last year   = −    $600   (made-up figure)
Conversion fee                            =  $10,632

What you save in the 18 months left of 24
Work in 18 months: $2,000 × 18           =  $36,000
Your fee: $36,000 × 10%                   =   $3,600
Tax on the fee: $3,600 × 18%              =     $648
Your saving                               =   $4,248

Compare
Fee $10,632 against your saving $4,248    →  not worth it

Break-even for your side alone
$10,632 ÷ 11.8%                           =  $90,102 of work
$90,102 ÷ 18 months                       =   $5,006 a month

The 11.8% is the 10% fee plus 18% tax on it. Maya also saves her own fees, so check her figure too.

The plain rule: below about $5,000 a month together, stay on Upwork. After 24 months the fee is $1, so a long relationship can simply wait.

Staying also keeps payment protection, reviews and Upwork's invoices. Those are worth a lot while you have no business setup.

Polite words for each request

"Let's talk on WhatsApp or Telegram." Before a contract:

Happy to talk. Let's keep it in Upwork chat for now; there's a call button here too. Once the contract starts, we can add whatever helps.

"What's your email?" Before a contract:

Upwork asks us to share contact details only after a contract starts. Send the offer and I'll share it right away for GitHub access.

"Can I pay you by PayPal or bank transfer?" At any time:

Thanks for thinking of my fees. I take payment only through Upwork, which protects us both. If you want to move off later, Upwork has a proper conversion option and I'm open to it.

Never sound accusing. Most clients ask from habit, not bad intent.

If you already said yes by mistake, fix it now. Write to her that you will keep all work and payment on Upwork. Then send no details and take no outside money.

That message is your record that you followed the rule. Keep it in the Upwork chat, where Upwork can see it.

Do this now

  • Save the three replies above in your notes.
  • Add one line to your proposal template rules: no chat app, email or PayPal before a contract.
  • Find the Report option in an Upwork chat so you know where it is.
  • Write on a note: "Leave Upwork only by conversion, only above $5,000 a month."
