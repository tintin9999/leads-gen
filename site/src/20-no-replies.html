Twenty proposals and no reply feels bad, but it is common for a new profile. This page gives you a 30-minute review of those 20. It finds which of four things is broken, in order, and names one change to make.

First, what is normal

A new profile with no reviews gets a reply to about 5 to 10 of every 100 proposals. That figure comes from tool sellers and freelancer reports, so treat it as rough.

Chance one proposal gets a reply:     about 7 in 100
Chance one proposal gets no reply:    93 in 100, or 0.93
Chance all 20 get no reply:           0.93 × 0.93 × ... (20 times) = 0.23
So: about 1 new freelancer in 4 sees zero replies in the first 20.

So zero from 20 can be plain bad luck. It can also point to something you can fix. The review below tells you which.

Do not react by sending more, faster. Do not cut your price to $10 an hour. Both make things worse.

The 30-minute review

You need your last 20 proposals and a sheet of paper. You pay to apply with tokens called Connects, so this review also saves money.

  1. Open Find work, then Proposals, and find your submitted and archived proposals.
  2. Open the first one. Open its job post in a second tab.
  3. Score the post again with the 8 signals from Pick jobs worth applying to.
  4. Read only the first two lines of your proposal. Are they about her problem, or about you?
  5. Note if the client viewed your proposal, where Upwork shows that.
  6. Note how the job ended: someone hired, nobody hired, or still open.
  7. Put one mark in each row of the table that applies.
  8. Repeat for all 20. Then add up each row.

Here is the table, filled in with a made-up result so you can see how it reads.

Read the result, in this order

Check the four causes from top to bottom. Stop at the first one that fits. That is your one change.

  1. The jobs you pick. Row A or row B is 6 or more. You applied to weak or crowded posts.
  2. The first two lines. Rows A and B are low, but row C is 6 or more, or row D is under 5.
  3. The profile. Row D is 8 or more, yet nobody replied. Clients opened your proposal, looked at you and left.
  4. The price. Everything above is fine, and row E is 6 or more.

In the table above, row A is 9 and row B is 11. So the first cause fits: the picks. You stop there.

You do not rewrite your profile or change your price yet. Only 4 clients even opened a proposal, so most never saw them.

Price comes last on purpose. New freelancers blame price first, and it is rarely the cause on well-chosen jobs.

The one change for the next 20

Make only the change for your cause. If you change four things at once, you cannot tell which one worked.

  • Picks: apply only to posts that score 7 or 8, are under 2 hours old and show fewer than 15 proposals. Send fewer if you must.
  • First two lines: start every proposal with her problem and your guess at the cause. Use the model in Write a proposal that gets a reply.
  • Profile: fix the title, the first two lines and one work sample that matches these jobs. See Write your Upwork profile.
  • Price: on small fixed-price jobs, bid the client's own budget. Do not go under $35 an hour.

A good pick looks like our made-up client Maya. Her post has verified payment, $4,000 spent, two clear bugs and was 40 minutes old.

Then send the next 20 and do this review again. Compare row D first. More views means the change is working, even before a reply comes.

While you wait

Replies often come days later, when a client's first choice fails. So keep your Upwork messages turned on in your phone.

Use the quiet hours for one small proof piece, such as a short write-up of a Supabase login fix. It gives your next proposal a better proof line.

If 60 careful proposals bring no reply at all, the cause may be the account, not you. Read Upwork rejected my profile or paused my account.

And if money is getting tight, read No work this month and savings are falling.

Do this now

  • Draw the six-row table on paper and review your last 20 proposals.
  • Circle the first cause that fits, from top to bottom.
  • Write your one change on a sticky note by your screen.
  • Set a reminder to repeat the review after the next 20.
