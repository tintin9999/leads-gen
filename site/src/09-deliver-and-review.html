Clients review how the job felt as much as the code. A client who always knew what was happening gives five stars. This page shows one 4-day job, day by day, from the start message to the review.

Maya's job, day by day

Maya is our made-up client, a US founder. Her $300 job has two funded steps (milestones): fix login for $120, then fix Stripe checkout for $180.

  • Day 0, contract starts. You send the start message below within the hour.
  • Day 1. You repeat both bugs on a test copy. You send a 2-minute video that shows them.
  • Day 2. You fix login and add a test. You submit step 1 for payment.
  • Day 3. You fix checkout and add tests for paid, failed and repeated payments.
  • Day 4. You hand over, submit step 2 and ask for the review.

The start message tells her what you need and when she will hear from you.

Thanks, Maya. I am starting now. I need two things: access to the GitHub repo, and a Stripe test key, not the live one.

You will get a short update from me every day by 9am your time. Step 1 is due Wednesday.

One short update a day

Send an update every working day, even when nothing is finished. Silence is what makes clients nervous.

Done today: found the login bug. The app saved the session in the wrong place, so Safari dropped it. Video attached.

Next: fix it and add a test tomorrow.

Need from you: nothing today.

Show progress she can see without reading code. A short video or a link to a test copy of the app works best.

If you are stuck or late, say so the same day. See I am stuck or will miss the deadline.

What you hand over

Your habits from Cultus are the handover: tests on money paths and written decisions. Few freelancers at this price give either.

  • The proposed code change in her repo (pull request), with a plain title.
  • The tests you added, and the one command that runs them.
  • A half-page note: what was wrong, what you changed, and why you chose that fix.
  • Three steps she can follow to check the fix herself.
  • A short list of risks you saw but did not touch. Do not fix them unasked.
  • A reminder to remove your access and replace any keys she shared.

The risk list often becomes your next job with her. Extra requests in the middle of this job are another matter: see The client keeps adding work.

Submit the work for payment

A file sent in chat does not start a payment. Only the button on the contract does.

  1. Open My jobs in the top menu and click Maya's contract.
  2. Click Submit work for payment.
  3. Choose the step and check the amount: $120 for step 1.
  4. Write two lines on what is done, and add the link or file.
  5. Click Submit.
  6. Message Maya: "Step 1 is submitted. Please approve it, and fund step 2 so I can go on."

Maya then has 14 days to approve the work or ask for changes, as of November 2026. When she approves, Upwork releases the held money to you.

If she does nothing for 14 days, Upwork releases the money to you without her. So a silent client does not mean lost money.

Do not submit the same work twice. Freelancers report that a second submission starts the 14 days again. Send one polite reminder on day 3 instead.

If she asks for changes, the count stops. Make the change if it is inside the agreed step, then submit again.

When the money reaches your bank is covered in How Upwork works.

Ask for the review and end the contract

Reviews happen only when the contract ends. Either of you can end it. Then each of you has 14 days to review the other.

Neither review shows until both are in, or the 14 days pass. So she cannot read yours first.

Do not end a fixed-price contract while a funded step is unpaid. The held money goes back to the client. Wait until the last step is paid.

Ask after she approves the last step, when she is pleased.

Thanks for approving, Maya. Everything is handed over, so you can end the contract when you are ready.

If the work helped, honest feedback would mean a lot. This is my first job on Upwork.

Ask for honest feedback, never for "five stars". Never offer a discount or extra work for a good review. Upwork treats that as breaking its feedback rules.

If she has not ended it after three days, end it yourself. Open the contract, choose End contract, and leave your review.

Maya gives two kinds of feedback. The public one is stars and a comment on your profile. The private one goes only to Upwork, and you never see it.

Both feed your Job Success Score, the percentage Upwork later shows on your profile. That is why the feel of the job matters so much.

Your review of her should be short, true and specific.

Maya explained both bugs clearly, gave access the same day and answered every question within hours. She approved and paid on time. I would gladly work with her again.

Do this now
