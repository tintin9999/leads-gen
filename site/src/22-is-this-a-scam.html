Some messages that look like work are traps. This page gives you a 60-second check before you reply. It also tells you exactly what to do if money or data has already gone.

Two messages, side by side

Message one arrives on Upwork, from a client with no hires and no reviews:

"Hi Parth, great profile! We pay $90 an hour for a React dashboard. Please add me on Telegram so our CTO can explain.

Before you start, pay a $25 setup fee for our project tool. You get it back with the first payment."

Message two comes from Maya, a made-up founder in the US. Her app "Fernleaf" was built with an AI app builder on Next.js and Supabase:

"Hi Parth, users on Fernleaf can see each other's orders. I think it is the Supabase rules.

Budget $300, fixed price. Can you look at the job post and tell me how you would start? Happy to answer questions here."

Message one pays far above normal and gives no real problem. It wants to leave Upwork before a contract. It asks you to pay.

Message two names a real problem and a normal budget. It stays on Upwork. Money only moves from her to you.

The 60-second check

Ask these six questions before you reply. One "yes" is a stop sign. End the talk and report it.

  1. Do they want to move to Telegram, WhatsApp or email before a contract exists?
  2. Do they ask you to pay anything: a fee, a deposit, equipment, software or gift cards?
  3. Do they want you to send money back, for any reason?
  4. Do they want to pay in crypto, or from someone else's account?
  5. Do they ask for your ID papers, bank login, one-time password (OTP) or Upwork login?
  6. Do they ask you to download and run their code on your own laptop before a contract?

Some signs are softer. A rate far above the market, a brand-new account, heavy hurry, or a vague job. Two or more of these means "slow down and ask more questions".

Most clients are honest. You will usually see one or two scam messages a week as a new profile. That is normal, not a sign you did something wrong.

The scams new freelancers in India meet

  • Off the platform: "Let's talk on Telegram." Upwork's rules forbid this before a contract. Off Upwork, you lose all payment protection.
  • Pay to start: a "registration", "training" or "verification" fee. Real clients never charge you to work. Upwork's own Connects are the only exception.
  • Overpayment: "We sent $2,000 by mistake instead of $200, please refund the extra." The first payment is fake or stolen and will bounce. Your refund is real.
  • Fake cheques and screenshots. A picture of a payment is not a payment. Only money that shows in your Upwork balance or bank counts.
  • Buy equipment. "Buy a laptop from our vendor, or gift cards, and we will pay you back." You will not be paid back.
  • Task jobs: "Like videos, earn ₹3,000 a day." The first tasks pay ₹150–500. Then you must deposit money to "unlock" bigger payouts that never come.
  • ID and OTP requests. Nobody needs your full Aadhaar, bank login or OTP to hire you. These are used for loans and fraud in your name.
  • Crypto payment. It cannot be reversed or traced. It is the scammers' favourite way to move money.
  • Renting your account: "We pay ₹20,000 a month to use your Upwork account." Upwork bans account sharing. It can also link you to crimes.
  • Run our code. A "test task" repository that steals your passwords when you install it. See A stranger asks you to run their code.

What to reply

For a soft warning sign, ask a real question and stay on Upwork:

"Thanks for reaching out. I keep all talks and payments on Upwork until a contract is in place. Could you share what the app does today and what is broken?"

For a stop sign, do not argue or explain. Send one line, or nothing:

"I don't work this way. Best of luck."

Then report it. In Upwork Messages, hover over the message, open the "…" menu and choose Report message. On a job post, use the flag button.

If money or data has already gone

Speed matters. A report in the first hour gives the bank the best chance to freeze the money.

  1. Stop. Send no more money, even if they promise a refund after "one last fee".
  2. Call 1930, India's national cyber-fraud helpline.
  3. File a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Add the transaction IDs, UPI IDs and screenshots of the chat.
  4. Call your bank's fraud line and ask them to block the transaction and your card if needed.
  5. If you shared a password or OTP, change that password now from a clean device. Turn on two-step login.
  6. Report the profile on Upwork, and block the number on WhatsApp or Telegram.
  7. Keep every message. Do not delete the chat, even if you feel embarrassed.

If you shared ID papers, watch your credit report (CIBIL or Experian) for loans you did not take. Smart people get caught too. Acting fast matters more than how it happened.

Do this now

  • Save 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in in your phone contacts.
  • Turn on two-step login on Upwork, your email and your bank app.
  • Download a masked Aadhaar (only the last 4 digits show) from myAadhaar, for any ID check.
  • Copy the six check questions into a note you can open while reading messages.
