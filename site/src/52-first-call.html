A direct client has agreed to a call. On this first call you ask and listen: you do not pitch or name a price. This page gives you the questions, the exact words, and the note you send afterwards.

What a good call sounds like

Asha runs "Indigo Thread", a made-up Pune clothing brand on Shopify. She answered your cold email. Ten minutes into a 20-minute video call, it goes like this:

You: What made you reply this week?

Asha: Our winter sale starts on 5 December. Last sale, people complained the site was slow on phones.

You: What did that cost you, as far as you can tell?

Asha: Ads cost more, and orders did not go up. I think we lost maybe one order in ten.

You: What have you tried so far?

Asha: Our agency compressed the photos. It helped a little. They are slow to answer now.

You: Tell me more about "slow to answer".

Notice what you did not do. You did not explain a fix or say a price. You asked short questions and wrote down her words.

Her words go straight into your proposal later. "People complained the site was slow on phones" is stronger than any sentence you could write.

Aim to talk less than half the time. Feeling nervous is normal: she just wants to know you understood her problem.

Before the call, and the opening

Spend five minutes before the call. Open her store on your phone and run Google PageSpeed Insights on one product page. Have your one-line Cultus sentence ready.

Then say the plan out loud. It calms both of you.

Thanks for your time, Asha. I will ask a few questions about the store, then tell you honestly whether I can help. Is that OK?

The five questions that matter

These five decide whether there is a project. Ask them in this order.

  1. The problem: "What made you look at this now?" Write the answer word for word.
  2. What it costs her: "What does this cost you today, in orders, hours or stress?"
  3. What she has tried: "What have you tried already, and what happened?"
  4. Who decides: "Apart from you, who is involved in deciding something like this?"
  5. Budget and timing: "Is there a date this must be done by?" Then ask about budget, as shown below.

When an answer is vague, say "Tell me more about that" or "How often?"

Help her put a number on the cost, using her own figures.

Her orders in a month       = 3,000
One order in ten lost       = 3,000 ÷ 10 = 300 orders
Average order               = ₹1,200
Sales lost in a sale month  = 300 × ₹1,200 = ₹3,60,000 (₹3.6 lakh)

You do not promise to win this back. The number just shows you both that the problem is worth fixing.

Asking about budget

Many people find this question the hardest. Ask it calmly, with a range, so she does not have to guess from nothing.

For an Indian client:

Projects like this usually land between ₹30,000 and ₹75,000, depending on how much we cover. Where would you want this to sit?

For a client abroad:

Work like this usually runs between $3,000 and $8,000, depending on scope. Is that roughly what you expected?

If she will not say, that is fine:

No problem. I will send three options, small, recommended and complete, each with a fixed price. You pick.

When she asks "What is your rate?"

Do not give an hourly rate on this call. She will compare it with cheaper people. Give a fixed price later, in writing, once you know the work.

Most of my work is fixed-price, so you know the full cost before we start. I will send three options by tomorrow evening, with what each includes.

If she pushes, repeat the range you already said. Never say your lowest rate out loud. How to build the options is on Quote a fixed price with three options.

End with a clear next step

First, play back what you heard, so she can correct you before you write anything.

Let me check I got this right. The store is slow on phones, and it matters because the sale starts on 5 December. Success means fast product pages on a phone before then.

Then fix the next step with a day and a time.

I will send three options by tomorrow at 6pm. Can we take 10 minutes on Thursday at 11am to go through them?

Send the calendar invite before you hang up. A call that ends with "I will get back to you" often goes nowhere.

Within one hour, send a short recap email. It shows you listened and gives you both a written record.

Subject: Recap: Indigo Thread call, 10 November

Hi Asha, thanks for the time today. Here is my summary. Please correct anything I got wrong.

Today: product pages are slow on phones, and customers complained during the last sale.

Why now: the winter sale starts on 5 December.

Success: fast product pages on a phone before the sale, measured before and after.

Who decides: you. Budget range we discussed: ₹30,000 to ₹75,000.

Next: three options by tomorrow at 6pm, and a walk-through on Thursday at 11am.

One thing would help: a list of the apps installed on your store.

Thanks, Parth

Indian clients often prefer WhatsApp. After the email, send one line there too: "Sent a short recap on email. Options coming tomorrow by 6pm."

Do this now

  • Save the opening and the five questions in a note on your phone.
  • Say the two budget lines out loud three times, until they feel normal.
  • Practise a 20-minute call with a friend playing Asha.
  • Save the recap email as a draft you can copy.
