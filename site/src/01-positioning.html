Bottom line. Don't sell "React/Next.js development". Sell outcomes to two buyers who already feel the pain:
  • Track A — Indian D2C brands on Shopify (cash now): faster stores, cleaner checkouts, integrations that don't drop orders. You built and run exactly this stack at Cultus, and its 12 brand partners are a warm door.
  • Track B — global founders with AI-built or rushed MVPs (rate growth): "Prototype → Production" — security, auth, payments, tests and deploys so they can launch to real users. Internal tools and Angular→React modernization are adjacent offers for the same buyers.
Headline everywhere: "Product engineer for commerce & internal tools — React, Next.js, Supabase, Shopify, Razorpay." Validate with 20 real conversations in three weeks, then double down on whichever track converts.

Why a niche, when you could do "anything React"

Generalist

"Full-stack dev, React/Node, available." Competes with every freelancer on Upwork and every agency in Pune. The buyer compares on price because nothing else is different.

price-driven

Specialist

"I fix slow Shopify stores for Indian D2C brands — mobile LCP under 2.5 s in 10 days." The buyer compares you to the cost of the problem, not to other freelancers.

value-driven

  • Referrals work: people can only refer you if they can say what you do in one sentence.
  • Outreach gets easier: one ICP means one list, one message, one case study.
  • Prices go up: specialists are paid for certainty and speed; generalists are paid for hours.
  • Delivery gets faster: the fifth speed audit takes half the time of the first, at the same price.
A niche is a marketing decision, not a cage. You can still take a good React project outside your niche. You just don't market yourself as a generalist.

Ideal customer profiles

ICP A — "Growing D2C brand on Shopify"
  • Who: Indian homegrown brand (fashion, beauty, home, food, wellness), 5–50 people, ₹1–50 Cr annual GMV, Shopify (sometimes Shopify Plus), selling on its site plus marketplaces.
  • Decision maker: founder or co-founder; sometimes a "growth/ops" lead. Pays from a marketing or tech budget.
  • Pains you can name: slow mobile pages (heavy themes, 20+ apps), checkout drop-off, COD-heavy orders and returns (RTO), inventory mismatches across channels, manual order ops, flaky third-party apps, monthly app bills that add up.
  • Signals: new collection/sale campaigns, hiring for "Shopify developer", Instagram-heavy growth, poor PageSpeed scores, many installed apps, recent funding on Inc42/Entrackr.
  • Where they are: Instagram, LinkedIn, D2C founder WhatsApp groups, Shopify partner/agency circles, Cultus.
  • Objections: "our agency handles it", "too expensive", "we'll do it after the sale".
ICP B1 — "Founder with an AI-built MVP"
  • Who: non-technical or semi-technical founder; MVP built with Lovable, Bolt, v0, Replit, Cursor or a cheap contractor; usually on Supabase/Firebase + Next.js/React; first paying users or a pilot coming.
  • Pains: "it works on my laptop", security holes (open database tables, keys in the frontend), broken auth flows, no tests, payments half-done, can't add features without breaking others, investor or enterprise-customer diligence.
  • Signals: posts like "need a developer to finish my Lovable app", Product Hunt/Show HN launches on these stacks, job posts for "fix my MVP".
  • Budget: $500–$10k; prefers fixed price; buys fast if trust is established.
  • Objections: "can't the AI just fix it?", "I'll find someone cheaper".
ICP B2 — "Ops-heavy company outgrowing spreadsheets"
  • Who: fintech, logistics, marketplace, healthcare-admin or B2B SaaS company with an operations team working in spreadsheets, Retool hacks or an old admin panel.
  • Pains: no permissions/audit trail, manual approvals, error-prone data entry, slow legacy panel, compliance asks.
  • Buyer: head of ops, CTO, or an engineering manager whose team has no frontend capacity.
  • Budget: $4k–$15k per build, then retainer.
ICP B3 — "Team stuck on legacy Angular"
  • Who: product companies with AngularJS (end of life since Jan 2022) or old Angular versions, small frontend team, hiring trouble because nobody wants to work on the old stack.
  • Offer: paid assessment (inventory, risk map, strangler-pattern plan, test harness), then module-by-module migration with Vitest in CI — exactly what you did at Deribit.
  • Signals: job posts mentioning "AngularJS" or "migrate to React", tech-stack lookups.

The offer ladder

Strangers don't buy a ₹3L project from someone they met yesterday. Give them a cheap, low-risk first step that naturally leads to the big one.

  1. Free value (5–15 min of your time) — a Loom teardown of their store speed or MVP security, a quick written list of three issues. Purpose: start a conversation.
  2. Paid diagnostic (₹15k–25k / $600–1,200) — audit + prioritized plan + estimate. Purpose: get paid to scope; 50–70% of good audits convert to the build.
  3. Core project (₹40k–₹4L / $3k–15k) — fixed scope, fixed price, three options.
  4. Care plan / retainer (₹25k–75k / $1k–3k per month) — maintenance, monitoring, small features, priority response.

Offer one-pager: A1 Store Speed & Conversion Sprint

For: D2C brands on Shopify whose mobile pages are slow or whose conversion dropped after adding apps.

Outcome: measurable improvement in mobile Core Web Vitals on the top templates (home, collection, product, cart), and a clean, documented theme.

Includes: baseline report (PageSpeed/CrUX where available, Lighthouse on key templates); app and third-party script audit with remove/replace/defer recommendations; image, font and hero-media fixes; Liquid/JS fixes in the theme; before/after report; 14-day watch period.

Guarantee idea: "If we don't improve mobile LCP on the product template, the second half of the fee is waived." Only offer this once you've done 2–3 sprints and know your numbers.

Upsell: C1 care plan to keep the store fast as apps get added; A3 checkout work.

Offer one-pager: B1 Prototype → Production

For: founders whose MVP was built with AI builders or in a rush and now needs to handle real users and money.

Audit (1 week, fixed fee): threat-model the data; check Supabase RLS policies, storage buckets, service-role key exposure, auth flows, input validation; review payment flows and webhooks; check error handling, logging, backups; add a smoke-test suite; deliver a red/amber/green report with a costed plan.

Sprint (2–4 weeks): fix the reds, add CI with tests on money and auth paths, staging environment, monitoring and alerts, ADRs for key decisions, handover call.

Why you: you've run a production marketplace on Next.js + Supabase with 115 test files and 17 ADRs, rewritten and scaled a legacy codebase (Bro), and you think like an attacker (CTF).

Messaging

Positioning statements

Track A: I help Indian D2C brands on Shopify sell more and break less — faster stores, cleaner checkouts and integrations that don't drop orders — because I built and run a live marketplace on exactly this stack.

Track B: I take AI-built and rushed MVPs to production — auth, data security, payments, tests and deploys — so founders can launch to real users without a rewrite.

Headlines (LinkedIn, website, platform profiles)

  • Product engineer for commerce & internal tools · React, Next.js, Supabase, Shopify, Razorpay
  • I build and fix the parts of your product that touch money: storefronts, checkouts, integrations, admin tools

Elevator pitches

10 seconds: "I'm a product engineer. I help D2C brands and startups with the parts of their product that touch money — stores, checkouts, integrations and admin tools."

30 seconds: "I spent four years building production React and TypeScript apps, most recently back-office tools for Deribit, the Coinbase-owned derivatives exchange. On the side I built Cultus, a live fashion marketplace with a custom Shopify Sales Channel app and Razorpay checkout. Now I help D2C brands make their stores faster and their checkouts and integrations reliable, and I help founders take AI-built MVPs to production. If you've got a slow store, a flaky integration or an MVP you're nervous to launch, that's my thing."

What you don't do (say it out loud)

  • No WordPress/Wix sites, no logo or brand design, no SEO-only retainers.
  • No hourly "staff augmentation" below your floor rate.
  • No equity-only work. No unpaid test projects beyond a 30-minute exercise.
  • No mobile-native apps (refer them to a partner and take a referral fee).

Name and brand

Recommendation: trade under your own name ("Parth Kulkarni — Product Engineering") with a short descriptor. Solo clients buy a person; a studio name adds nothing until you subcontract. A sole proprietorship can still use a trade name on invoices later if you want one; see Business Setup.

  • Domain: your name (.com, .dev or .in). Use a professional email on it.
  • Visual identity: one accent colour, one typeface, a good photo. That's enough.

Common positioning mistakes

  • Listing technologies instead of outcomes. Buyers search for "slow Shopify store", not "Liquid + Lighthouse".
  • Too many offers at once. Launch with two (A1 and B1). Add the rest as you get asked.
  • Hiding the proof. Lead with Cultus numbers and the Deribit/Coinbase name (within your NDA).
  • Changing positioning every week. Give it 90 days of consistent outreach before judging.
  • Pricing like an employee. Your monthly salary ÷ 160 hours is the wrong maths; see Pricing.

Actions

  • Pick the two launch offers (default: A1 Store Speed Sprint and B1 Prototype → Production audit).
  • Write your positioning statements in your own words and read them aloud to two friends.
  • Write the "what I don't do" list and keep it next to your desk.
  • Run free PageSpeed tests on 10 Cultus-partner and friend-brand stores to calibrate the A1 pitch.
  • Do one free B1-style teardown of a public AI-built app (with permission) to calibrate the audit.
  • Create the A1 and B1 presets in the Proposal Builder with your numbers.
