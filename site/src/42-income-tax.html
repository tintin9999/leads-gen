At your level, income tax is small, and in your first year it is probably zero. This page works out your real first year line by line. Then it gives the few dates that matter.

Your first year, worked out

The tax year runs from April 2026 to March 2027. You were paid a salary by Noovosoft from April to September: ₹7.5 lakh. From October to March, say you receive ₹8 lakh from freelance work.

Salary, April to September                   ₹7,50,000
Salary deduction (fixed, for salaried people) − ₹75,000
Salary counted for tax                      = ₹6,75,000

Freelance receipts                           ₹8,00,000
Half counted as profit:  ₹8,00,000 × 50%    = ₹4,00,000

Total income:  ₹6,75,000 + ₹4,00,000        = ₹10,75,000

Tax by slab
  first ₹4,00,000 at 0%                      =       ₹0
  ₹4,00,000 to ₹8,00,000:  ₹4,00,000 × 5%    =  ₹20,000
  ₹8,00,000 to ₹10,75,000: ₹2,75,000 × 10%   =  ₹27,500
  tax before rebate                         =  ₹47,500
Rebate (total income ₹12 lakh or less)       − ₹47,500
Tax to pay                                  =       ₹0

So on ₹14.25 lakh of money in hand, your income tax for this year is ₹0.

The "half counted as profit" line is a simple scheme for professionals (presumptive taxation, section 58 of the Income-tax Act 2025, the old section 44ADA). You declare 50% of your receipts as profit. You do not list expenses like your laptop or software, because the other half is treated as covering them.

You may use it while your receipts stay under ₹75 lakh a year and at most 5% come in cash. Bank transfers and Upwork payments are not cash, so you qualify.

The salary deduction of ₹75,000 is only for salary. Freelance money does not get it.

The rebate is a cut that wipes out the tax when total income is ₹12 lakh or less. These are the new-regime rules for this year, as of November 2026. Stay in this regime unless a chartered accountant shows you a reason not to.

A bigger year: ₹36 lakh of receipts

Now a later year with no salary, where you receive ₹36 lakh from clients.

Receipts                                     ₹36,00,000
Half counted as profit:  ₹36,00,000 × 50%   = ₹18,00,000

Tax by slab
  first ₹4,00,000 at 0%                      =        ₹0
  ₹4,00,000 to ₹8,00,000 at 5%               =   ₹20,000
  ₹8,00,000 to ₹12,00,000 at 10%             =   ₹40,000
  ₹12,00,000 to ₹16,00,000 at 15%            =   ₹60,000
  ₹16,00,000 to ₹18,00,000 at 20%            =   ₹40,000
  tax before cess                           = ₹1,60,000
No rebate (income above ₹12 lakh)
Health and education cess:  4% of ₹1,60,000 =    ₹6,400
Tax to pay                                  = ₹1,66,400

That is about 4.6% of what you received. The rule of thumb: up to about ₹24 lakh of receipts in a year, with no other income, your tax is zero.

Just above that line, a small amount of tax starts. At ₹25 lakh it is about ₹52,000. You can try your own numbers in the rate calculator.

When money counts, and tax held back

Count each Upwork payment in the tax year you receive it. Use the rupee amount that reaches your bank, plus the small tax Upwork held back.

Upwork holds back 0.1% as income tax. This is called tax deducted at source (TDS). It is not lost: it counts as tax you have already paid.

Later, an Indian company pays you ₹1,00,000 for a project. It is their third invoice from you this year.

Your invoice                                ₹1,00,000
TDS held back by the client: 10%           − ₹10,000
Paid into your bank                         = ₹90,000
The ₹10,000 shows against your PAN as tax already paid
Your tax for the year is ₹0, so ₹10,000 comes back as a refund

Indian business clients hold back 10% for professional work, sometimes 2% for technical work. They must do it once they pay you more than ₹50,000 in a year. Always invoice the full amount and let them hold back the tax.

To get the credit, put your PAN on every invoice. Check that your PAN is linked to Aadhaar, or the rate jumps to 20%.

Every few months, open your yearly tax statement on the income tax website. It lists all TDS against your PAN. If a client's TDS is missing, ask them to file it.

One payment date: 15 March

Tax paid during the year is called advance tax. Under the simple scheme you pay all of it in one go, by 15 March.

You only pay if your tax for the year, after TDS, is ₹10,000 or more. In your first year it is ₹0, so there is likely nothing to pay.

  1. On 1 March, add up your receipts for the year so far, and estimate the rest.
  2. Work out the tax as in the examples above.
  3. Subtract the TDS shown in your yearly tax statement.
  4. If ₹10,000 or more is left, go to incometax.gov.in and choose e-Pay Tax, then Advance Tax.
  5. Pay from your tax savings, and save the payment receipt in your Tax folder.

If you pay late, interest of about 1% a month is added. It is small, but avoidable.

Your return and its due date

Your yearly income tax return (ITR) for April 2026 to March 2027 goes on form ITR-4, also called Sugam. It fits salary plus income under the simple scheme, up to ₹50 lakh of total income.

The due date is expected to be 31 August 2027. Confirm it when the forms come out.

Ask a chartered accountant to pick the right business code on your first ITR. A fee of a few thousand rupees is worth it for the first one.

The two deadlines that matter now

  • 31 December 2026: the last date to file the ITR for April 2025 to March 2026, your salaried year, if you have not filed it. The late fee is ₹5,000, or ₹1,000 if income was ₹5 lakh or less. Use the salary form from Noovosoft.
  • 15 March 2027: advance tax for this year, only if ₹10,000 or more is due after TDS.

If Noovosoft held back tax on your salary as if you worked all year, you will get some back as a refund. It comes after you file the August 2027 ITR.

Do this now

  • Check on incometax.gov.in that your ITR for April 2025 to March 2026 was filed. If not, file it before 31 December.
  • Check that your PAN is linked to Aadhaar.
  • Put a reminder in your calendar for 1 March to estimate the year.
  • Keep a simple list of every payment you receive, with date and rupee amount.
