Your warmest direct clients are Indian clothing and lifestyle brands on Shopify, like the brands on Cultus. This page shows who they are, what they worry about, and how to reach them. It is Stage 2 work: read it now, act on it later.

One store, one offer

Asha runs "Indigo Thread", a clothing brand in Pune. She is made up, but her numbers are typical, and her Shopify store gets about 3,000 orders a month. The average order is ₹1,200, and 60% of orders are paid in cash on delivery.

On a phone, her product page takes 5.8 seconds to show its main image. Google calls this time the "largest contentful paint" (LCP). It counts 2.5 seconds or less as good.

You offer her the Store Speed Sprint, middle size, for ₹48,000 over 10 days. She gets:

  • A measured "before" report of her home, collection and product pages on mobile.
  • Removal or delay of slow app scripts across the whole store.
  • Smaller images and fonts, and fixes in the theme code.
  • An "after" report with the same tests, so she sees the change in numbers.
  • 30 days of watching the speed, and a short handover note.
Her sales in a month = 3,000 orders × ₹1,200
                     = ₹36,00,000 (₹36 lakh)
Your price as a share = ₹48,000 ÷ ₹36,00,000
                     = 1.3% of one month's sales

The offer works because it names her problem, a fixed price and a clear result. You promise measured speed, not more sales. More sales depend on things you do not control, like her ads and prices.

The sprint has a smaller size at ₹30,000 and a bigger one at ₹75,000. How to present all three is on Quote a fixed price with three options.

Who these buyers are and what they worry about

Your buyer is usually the founder of a brand with 5 to 50 people. The brand sells on its own Shopify store, on Instagram, and often on marketplaces too. The founder decides fast and pays from a marketing budget.

Their worries are practical:

  • Slow mobile pages, often from a heavy theme and 20 or more apps.
  • Cash-on-delivery orders that are refused and shipped back. This is called "return to origin" (RTO), and each one costs shipping both ways.
  • Stock counts that do not match between Shopify and the marketplaces.
  • An agency that built the store and now answers slowly.
  • Breaking the checkout during a sale.

The festive season runs from about September to November, and there are end-of-season sales too. Sell your work before a sale. Never change a live store in the 7 days before one.

Why your Cultus work is the proof

These founders cannot judge your code. They can judge whether you have done their kind of work before. You have.

You built Cultus, a live fashion marketplace on Next.js and Supabase. It has a custom Shopify app that syncs stock every minute, plus a nightly check. Its checkout uses Razorpay Magic Checkout with part-payment for cash on delivery.

Say it in one line: "I built and run Cultus, a fashion marketplace with 12 brand partners, a custom Shopify app and Razorpay checkout."

Never use what you know about Cultus brands, such as their sales, to pitch or price. Use only public numbers and your own work.

How to reach them, in this order

  1. Ask your Cultus co-founders, in writing, if you may offer your services to the brand partners. Wait for a written yes. The rules are on Rules from your old job and from Cultus.
  2. Offer each brand partner a free 20-minute store check that ends with three concrete fixes.
  3. Ask each brand you help for two introductions to other founders.
  4. Message founders you already know: friends, old classmates, people from Pune events.
  5. For strangers, run Google PageSpeed Insights on their store on mobile.
  6. Record a 3-minute screen video that shows the brand its own speed numbers and the first fixes you would make.
  7. Send it in a short email, as shown on Write a cold email people answer.

Only look at what any shopper can see. Never test a store's systems without written permission.

What Indian clients expect

WhatsApp first: many founders will message you on WhatsApp and send voice notes. That is normal. After each decision, send a one-line email so there is a written record.

Bargaining: most will ask for a lower price. Do not add padding to your price to leave room. Offer a smaller option instead, as shown on The client wants it cheaper, or free.

Tax held back: a business client may hold back part of your fee as income tax and pay it to the government for you. This is "tax deducted at source" (TDS).

As of November 2026, it is 10% for professional services, once a client pays you more than ₹50,000 in a year. On ₹1,00,000 you get ₹90,000 now. The other ₹10,000 counts toward your own tax when you file.

Deposit first: ask for 50% before you start and 50% on delivery. Indian founders accept this when you say it early and calmly.

Why this is Stage 2, not week 1

On Upwork, the platform gives you the contract, sends the invoice and holds the client's money. With a direct client, you do all of that yourself. That is a lot to learn while you look for your first job.

So start here after about five good Upwork reviews. You do not need this yet. In Stage 1, your only outside work is the slow backup: five emails a week.

Do this now

  • Write your one-line Cultus sentence and keep it in your notes.
  • Run PageSpeed Insights on mobile for three stores you know, so the numbers feel familiar.
  • List the founders you know who run an online brand. Do not message them yet.
  • Put a reminder in your calendar for Stage 2: "Ask co-founders about brand partners."
