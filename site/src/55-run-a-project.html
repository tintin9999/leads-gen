A direct client cannot see your screen. What makes her feel safe is a fixed rhythm: a kickoff, an update every Friday, a demo, and a clean handover. This page walks through one two-week project, step by step.

Asha's two weeks on one page

Asha runs "Indigo Thread", a made-up Pune clothing brand on Shopify. She signed the one-page agreement for the Store Speed Sprint at ₹48,000.

Her winter sale starts on 5 December. Here is the whole project:

  1. Mon 16 Nov: the deposit of ₹24,000 reaches your account. You confirm on WhatsApp and book the kickoff.
  2. Tue 17 Nov: 30-minute kickoff call. You agree the goal, the dates and who answers questions.
  3. Tue 17 Nov: she gives you staff access to the store. You never ask for her own password.
  4. Fri 20 Nov: your first weekly update by email.
  5. Wed 25 Nov: a 15-minute demo of the faster pages, on her phone.
  6. Fri 27 Nov: second weekly update. She replies "Approved". You send the final invoice for ₹24,000, due in 7 days.
  7. When it is paid: you send the handover pack and remove your access.

Write these dates into the kickoff notes and send them to her. A client who knows what happens next does not chase you.

The rest of this page explains each part in a few lines.

The kickoff call

Keep it to 30 minutes, and send the agenda the day before. Cover four things:

  • The goal in one sentence: "Product pages load in under 3 seconds on a mid-range phone, before 5 December."
  • The dates above, and what you need from her by when.
  • One person who answers your questions, and how fast. For Asha: WhatsApp, same day.
  • How access is given safely.

Safe access means she adds you as staff with only the rights you need (in Shopify: Settings, then Users). Each person keeps their own login. Never accept passwords over WhatsApp.

The Friday update

Every Friday, send a short written update, even when little happened. Ten minutes is enough. Here is Asha's first one:

Subject: Indigo Thread speed sprint, week 1 update (20 Nov). On track.

Done: product pages now load in 3.4 seconds on a phone, down from 7.1. I removed two unused apps and resized the main photos.

Next week: the collection pages and the cart, then the demo on Wednesday at 4pm.

Needed from you: a yes to remove the old reviews app, by Monday. If this slips, the dates move by the same number of days.

Scope and budget: on budget, no extra work.

Have a good weekend, Parth

Use a status word at the top: on track, at risk, or off track. If you are at risk, say why and what you will do.

Bad news on a Friday is calm. Bad news on the last day is a fight.

If she asks for extra things mid-project, see The client keeps adding work. If you are falling behind, see I am stuck or will miss the deadline.

The full format is in the weekly update template.

Approval and the 30-day fix period

At the demo, show the result on her own phone. Then send a short list of what was promised, each marked done, with before and after numbers.

The agreement gives her 5 working days to approve or list what fails. Ask for one word in writing:

Everything in the agreement is done and live. Could you reply "Approved" if you are happy? Then I will send the final invoice.

Her written approval (acceptance) ends the build. Anything new after this is a new piece of work with its own price.

For 30 days after approval, you fix anything that does not work as promised, at no charge. This is the fix period. Write "30 days" into the agreement.

A fix is something promised that is broken. A new idea, like a new page design, is extra work. Say this kindly, and give a price.

The handover pack

Once the final invoice is paid, send the handover pack. For a speed sprint, it is short:

  • A list of every change you made, with before and after speed numbers.
  • Any code saved in her accounts, not yours.
  • A 5-minute screen recording that walks through the changes.
  • A note that you removed your staff access, and which passwords she should change.
  • The date the fix period ends.

Ask her to reply "Handover received", so you both have a record. Use the handover checklist for bigger projects.

Four things to ask for at the end

A happy client at handover is your best chance of the next job. Ask for these four, in one message, a few days after payment:

  1. A short quote for your website: two or three sentences in her words.
  2. Permission to write up the work as a case study. She sees it before it goes live.
  3. An introduction to one other brand owner who might have the same problem.
  4. Monthly upkeep (a care plan), from ₹25,000 a month, so the store stays fast.

Most clients say yes to one or two. That is normal. The steps are in the closeout template.
