After any first contact, a direct client usually looks you up on LinkedIn. Your profile should say in one glance what you fix, with proof. Then 20 minutes a day keeps you in front of the people who could hire you.

Your headline and the top of your About

Here is a headline built from your one sentence and your real work:

I fix and finish web apps built with Next.js, React and Supabase · Payments, login security, admin dashboards · Built Cultus · Ex-Deribit (Coinbase) back office

And the first three lines of your "About" section. These are the only lines people see before they click "see more":

I fix and finish web apps built with Next.js, React and Supabase.

I built Cultus, a live fashion marketplace: checkout, Shopify inventory sync, 180+ orders in its first four months.

Before that, I built back-office tools for Deribit, owned by Coinbase.

A visitor now knows what you do, and has two reasons to believe it. That is the whole job of the top of the page.

Most weak profiles list skills instead of results. Here is one line, before and after:

  • Before: "Passionate full-stack developer with 4 years of experience in React, Node and more."
  • After: "I built the payments and the Shopify sync for Cultus, with 115 tests on the money paths."

The second line names a real project and a number. Anyone could write the first.

The LinkedIn profile template has an older, broader headline. Use the one-sentence headline above instead, and take the rest of the template as a starting point.

Fix five parts in one sitting

Set aside one evening, about two hours. Do the parts in this order:

  1. Headline: paste the headline above. Read it once on your phone to check it is not cut off badly.
  2. About: start with the three lines above. Then add what you help with, how you work (fixed prices, weekly updates), and a link to parthkulkarni.dev.
  3. Featured: add your Cultus case study page from parthkulkarni.dev first. Add a second case study next. Later, add your best post.
  4. Experience: under Noovosoft, write one result-first line each for Deribit and Twinntax. Add Cultus as its own entry. Add "Independent developer" from 2026.
  5. Photo and banner: a clear, friendly face photo with plain light. A simple banner with your one sentence and your site address.

Then turn on the free services page.

Go to your profile, click Open to, then Providing services. Choose web development, and set Pune with remote work. Paste a short description built from your one sentence.

This page lets people find you when they search for a developer. When a request comes in, reply within one day.

Ask five people for a recommendation

A written recommendation from a real colleague is strong proof for a stranger. Five is enough to start. Ask these people:

  • Your manager at Noovosoft.
  • One senior colleague from the Deribit or Twinntax work.
  • Two of the junior developers you mentored.
  • One brand partner from Cultus.

Send each one a personal message, with one specific thing they could mention:

Hi Rohan, I have started working independently, fixing and finishing web apps. Would you be open to writing a short LinkedIn recommendation about our work on the Deribit admin tools?

Even two or three sentences on the Angular to React move would help a lot. Happy to write one for you too. Thanks!

(Rohan is a made-up name: use the real person's.) Most people say yes but forget. A friendly reminder after a week is normal.

The 20-minute daily habit

Once the profile is done, spend 20 minutes a day, at the same time. Set a timer and stop when it rings.

  1. Spend 15 minutes writing ten useful comments on posts by people who could hire you. For example, founders of Indian clothing brands on Shopify, or founders building apps with AI app builders.
  2. Spend 5 minutes replying to every comment and message you received.

A useful comment adds one fact or one question. "Great post!" adds nothing.

We hit the same thing on Cultus: Shopify stock updates sometimes arrived late during bulk imports. A small nightly check that compares both sides fixed the gaps.

Once a week, write one short post from your real work. Pick from this list, one per week:

  • How Cultus keeps stock in sync with Shopify every minute, plus a nightly check.
  • Why Cultus has 115 tests on the money paths, and which bug they caught.
  • Three things to check before an AI-built app on Supabase goes live.
  • What moving old Angular screens to React taught you at Deribit.
  • How you scaled the Bro Discord bot to 10,000+ servers.
  • Building tax forms that change every year, from the Twinntax work.

Most early posts get few likes. That is normal.

The post is there for the visitor who checks your profile later. More ideas are in the content calendar.

What not to do

  • No "open to work" photo frame. It tells clients you want a job, not their project.
  • No mass connection requests. Sending many requests to strangers can get your account limited. Connect with people you comment with, and add one personal line.
  • No pitch in a first message. Ask a question about their work instead.
