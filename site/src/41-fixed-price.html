A fixed price is one total for one clear piece of work. You build it from hours and your rate, add room for surprises, and offer it in three sizes. This page builds one real quote line by line.

Build one quote, line by line

The Store Speed Sprint makes an Indian Shopify store load faster. Here is the full job, with your estimate for each task.

Tasks and hours:
  check the store and measure speed            4 hours
  remove slow apps and scripts                 5 hours
  fix images and fonts                         5 hours
  fix the theme code                           7 hours
  write the report and hand over               3 hours
  total                                     = 24 hours

Hours × rate:   24 × ₹1,800               = ₹43,200
Add 25% for surprises:  ₹43,200 × 1.25    = ₹54,000
Add 10% for calls and updates:  × 1.10    = ₹59,400

Price for the full list                   ≈ ₹59,400

The ₹1,800 is your normal rate for Indian clients, not your floor. The two extra lines pay for real time you would otherwise lose.

  • The safety margin covers surprises: old code, a slow app, a missing password. Use 20% for work you know well and 40% for messy old code.
  • The 10% covers calls, messages, progress updates and the final handover. Clients rarely see these hours, but you spend them.

Turn it into three sizes

Now cut the task list into a small, a middle and a large option. Price each the same way.

Essential:   12 hours × ₹1,800 × 1.25 × 1.10 = ₹29,700  → offer ₹30,000
Recommended: 20 hours × ₹1,800 × 1.25 × 1.10 = ₹49,500  → offer ₹48,000
Premium:     30 hours × ₹1,800 × 1.25 × 1.10 = ₹74,250  → offer ₹75,000
  • Essential, ₹30,000, 7 days: the check, the top fixes on the home and product pages, and a before-and-after report.
  • Recommended, ₹48,000, 10 days: everything in Essential, plus slow apps and scripts removed across the store, image and font fixes, 30 days of speed checks and a handover note.
  • Premium, ₹75,000, 14 days: everything in Recommended, plus the theme code fixes, quick wins in cart and checkout, and 60 days of care.

The middle price is rounded down to stay under ₹50,000. It still pays ₹2,400 an hour, well above your floor of ₹1,200.

Why three options work

With one price, the client asks "yes or no?" Many say no, or ask for a discount.

With three prices, the client asks "which one?" That is an easier question, and most people pick the middle.

Mark the middle option "Recommended" and make it the one you believe fits best. The small option gives a careful client a way in. The large one lets a bigger client buy more without a second round of talks.

If a client wants a lower price, move them to a smaller option. Do not cut the price of the same work. The page The client wants it cheaper, or free gives the words.

Check the price against what the problem costs

Before you send a quote, ask what the problem costs the client each month. A price is easy to accept when it is small next to that cost.

A made-up Shopify brand loses money on failed cash-on-delivery orders. The buyer refuses the parcel, and it comes back.

Orders a month                               3,000
Paid by cash on delivery:  60%             = 1,800 orders
Refused and sent back:     25% of 1,800    = 450 orders
Cost of each return (shipping, packing)      ₹200
Monthly loss:  450 × ₹200                  = ₹90,000
If new checkout rules cut returns by 20%:
  ₹90,000 × 0.20                           = ₹18,000 saved a month
  ₹18,000 × 12                             = ₹2,16,000 saved a year
Your checkout offer (large size)             ₹75,000
Months to pay back:  ₹75,000 ÷ ₹18,000     ≈ 4 months

Ask for the client's own numbers on the call: orders a month, share paid in cash, share returned. Industry blogs put returns at 20–35%, so treat the 25% as a rough guide.

The same method on Upwork

Many Upwork jobs ask for one fixed-price bid. Build it the same way, in dollars.

Maya, a made-up founder in the US, needs the login and data rules fixed in her app Fernleaf.

Estimate:   6 hours × $40                 = $240
Add 25% for surprises:  $240 × 1.25       = $300
Add 10% for calls and updates:  × 1.10    = $330
Bid                                        $330
Upwork fee of 10%, plus 18% tax on it:
  you keep 88.2%:  $330 × 0.882           = $291.06

Put the middle size in the bid box. In the proposal text, mention a smaller and a larger option in one line each. Build the bid and proposal in the proposal builder.

Do this now

  • Write the task list and hours for one job you could sell this week.
  • Price it: hours × rate, then add 25% and 10%.
  • Cut it into three sizes and mark the middle one "Recommended".
  • Save the three sizes in the proposal builder.
  • After each job, divide the price by the hours you really spent. Compare it with your floor.
