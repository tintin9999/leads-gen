Upwork is your main road. This page sets up one small side road: five personal messages a week. It may bring one lucky client or nothing for months, and both are fine.

One week of the backup

Here is a full week. Each message takes about 15 minutes.

  • Monday: an old teammate from Noovosoft who moved to a startup.
  • Tuesday: a developer you mentored two years ago.
  • Wednesday: a college friend whose family runs a business.
  • Thursday: a founder you met at a Pune meetup.
  • Friday: a short follow-up to last week's Monday message.

That is the whole backup: five messages, about 75 minutes in the week.

It looks too small to matter. It is small on purpose, so that it never steals time from Upwork.

Over eight weeks it still adds up to 40 people who know what you do now.

Start with people who know you

People who have seen your work already trust you. A stranger has to be convinced from zero.

The numbers are rough, and they show the gap. About one in three people you know will reply. For strangers it is closer to one in thirty.

So you begin with a list of 30 names. Use these four groups to fill it:

  • Old teammates and managers.
  • Developers you mentored or interviewed.
  • College friends who now work at startups or run a business.
  • People you know through Cultus and its brand partners.

Put the ten people most likely to know founders at the top. Write to them first.

Do not sort the list for days. A rough order is enough, and the first message matters more than the perfect list.

Wait before you write to anyone at Deribit or Twinntax. First read Rules from your old job and from Cultus.

The message to send

You are not asking these people for work. You are asking them to remember you when they hear of a problem.

Hi {{name}}, a quick update from my side. I left Noovosoft in September and now work for myself.

I fix and finish web apps built with Next.js, React and Supabase. Mostly login, payment and dashboard problems.

If you hear of a founder or a team stuck with something like that, could you point them my way? No rush at all. How are things with you?

Three things make this message work.

  • It is short. It can be read on a phone in 20 seconds.
  • It has one small ask. "Point them my way" is easy to say yes to.
  • It ends with a question about them. That keeps it a conversation and not an advert.

Change the first line for each person. Mention one thing you did together, so it does not read like a mass message.

One public post

In week 1, also write one short post on LinkedIn. It tells everyone at once that you now work for yourself.

Keep it to four lines: what changed, what you fix, one real project as proof, and how to reach you.

You do not need to post again every day. The page LinkedIn in 20 minutes a day covers the rest, and it can wait.

Strangers come second

Your list of 30 names lasts about seven weeks, because one message a week is a follow-up.

After that, the five weekly messages go to strangers. This is called cold email: an email to someone who does not know you.

A cold email only works when it is about their problem. The page Write a cold email people answer shows one, line by line.

The limit stays the same: five a week. More than that, and each one gets worse.

One more portal

The last part of the backup is a profile on one other portal. You set it up once, in week 2, and then leave it.

It is a net. If a client finds you there, good. You do not hunt for jobs on it yet.

The page Other portals: which and when names the one to pick and the ones to skip for now.

Keep it small on purpose

The backup stays useful only if it stays small. These rules protect your Upwork time.

  1. Do your two Upwork hours first, every day.
  2. Then send one backup message. Stop after one.
  3. Note each message in the pipeline tracker, with a date seven days later.
  4. On that date, send one short follow-up.
  5. If there is still no reply, leave it. Do not write a third time.

When someone replies with a name, answer the same day. Ask them to introduce you in a short message or email.

Then treat that new person like any client. Listen to the problem first, and do not push a price in the first message.

Never use a tool that sends messages for you. People can tell, and it harms your name.

If the backup brings nothing in Stage 1, that is normal. Its real value shows later, when someone remembers you.

Do this now

  • Write a list of 30 people who know your work.
  • Send the first message today, with a first line written only for that person.
  • Add that person to the pipeline tracker with a follow-up date.
  • Add a repeating weekly task to your calendar: "5 backup messages".
