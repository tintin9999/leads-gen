A client has answered your proposal. This page walks you from that reply to the first hour of work. One rule runs through it: no work before a contract, and no work before Upwork holds the money.

Answer fast and offer a call

Maya is our made-up client, a US founder with a $300 job: fix login and Stripe checkout in her app "Fernleaf".

At 8:10pm in Pune she writes: "Thanks, only some cards leave orders pending. Can you start this week?"

You answer by 8:30pm.

Thanks, Maya. "Only some cards" points to cards that need an extra bank check, so that helps a lot. Yes, I can start tomorrow.

Could we do a 15-minute call here on Upwork first? I am free today until 11am your time, or tomorrow from 9am.

Clients often talk to two or three freelancers. The first clear answer usually wins. Reply within an hour when she writes in your waking hours.

Hold the call inside Upwork. Open the chat in Messages and use the call button there. Do not send your own meeting link before a contract.

The short call: five questions

The call has one goal. You both agree what "done" means. Ask these five questions and write down her answers.

  1. "Can you share your screen and show me what a user sees when it breaks?"
  2. "When did it start, and what changed just before?"
  3. "Who uses the app today: testers, or paying customers?"
  4. "How will you check that it is fixed?"
  5. "Is there a test copy of the app, and who gives me access?"

End by saying what happens next: "I will send a two-step plan in the chat in 30 minutes."

Being nervous on a first call is normal. She is not testing your English. She wants to know if you understood her problem.

Hourly or fixed price for a job like this

Upwork has two kinds of contract. In a fixed-price contract you agree one price for a named result. In an hourly contract she pays for each hour you track.

Maya's job has two named bugs and a way to check each one. That suits a fixed price of $300.

A job like "find out why my app is slow" has no clear end. That suits hourly, at $35 to $40 an hour.

For your first three jobs, choose small fixed-price jobs when the task is clear. You get a review sooner, and the client knows the cost.

Split the price into funded steps

A fixed price can be split into steps, each with its own payment (milestones). Before each step starts, the client pays that amount to Upwork. Upwork holds it (escrow) until she approves your work.

Step 1: fix login, with a test          $120   due day 2
Step 2: fix checkout, tests, handover   $180   due day 4
Total:  $120 + $180 = $300

You send this plan in the chat after the call.

Here is the plan we agreed. Step 1, $120: login stays signed in on Chrome and Safari. Step 2, $180: every paid order is marked paid, with handover notes.

Please send the offer with these two steps and fund step 1. I start the moment it shows as funded.

Small steps protect you both. If something goes wrong, only one step is in doubt, not the whole job.

Check the offer before you accept

The client now sends an offer: the contract waiting for your yes. Upwork emails you, and the offer shows in your messages.

  1. Open the offer and read the type: fixed price or hourly.
  2. Check the total and each step against your plan: $120 and $180.
  3. Read the description. It must match what you agreed, with nothing added.
  4. Check the due dates. Ask for a change if a date is wrong.
  5. Note the Upwork fee shown for this contract.
  6. If all is right, click Accept offer. If not, message her and ask her to change it.
  7. Open the contract page. Check that step 1 shows as funded.

Asking for a correction is normal. Clients often fill in the form quickly and make small mistakes.

Two red lines

Red line 1: no work before the money is held. On a fixed price, start only when the step shows as funded. A promise in chat is not money.

On an hourly job, start only after the contract begins and the Upwork desktop app is tracking your time. Hours you type in by hand are not covered by Upwork's payment protection.

This also means no "quick free look" at her code before the contract. See The client wants it cheaper, or free.

Red line 2: no contact or payment outside Upwork. Before a contract, do not share email, phone, WhatsApp or Telegram. At no time take payment outside Upwork.

After the contract starts, Upwork lets you share contact details, for example an email for GitHub access. Still keep decisions written in Upwork chat. That record protects you.

If she asks to move off Upwork, read The client wants to leave Upwork. If something feels wrong, read Is this client a scam?

Do this now

  • Save the five call questions in a note on your phone.
  • Install the Upwork desktop app and sign in, so hourly tracking is ready.
  • Make one test call with a friend, using your headset and screen share.
  • Write the two red lines on paper and keep them by your desk.
