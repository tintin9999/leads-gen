After a good first call, send a written proposal within one day. It gives three options with fixed prices. Then follow up on fixed days, so you never have to wonder whether to write again.

Asha's proposal, in outline

Asha runs "Indigo Thread", a made-up Pune clothing brand on Shopify. Your call was on Tuesday, 10 November. On Wednesday by 6pm she gets a two-page proposal with these parts.

1. Her problem, in her words: "People complained the site was slow on phones during the last sale."

Product pages take 5.8 seconds to show their main content on a phone. Google counts 2.5 seconds or less as good. The winter sale starts on 5 December.

2. Three options:

  • Essential, ₹30,000, 7 days: a speed check, the top fixes on home and product pages, and a before-and-after report.
  • Recommended, ₹48,000, 10 days: all of Essential, plus slow apps removed across the store. It adds image and font fixes, 30 days of speed checks and a handover note.
  • Premium, ₹75,000, 14 days: all of Recommended, plus theme code fixes, quick wins in cart and checkout, and 60 days of care.

3. Not included: a new theme, new features, and any change in the 7 days before the sale.

4. Timeline: start within 2 days of the deposit; finish by 27 November for Recommended, a week before the sale.

5. Payment: 50% before work starts, 50% on delivery, each due within 7 days of the invoice.

6. Valid for 14 days: until 25 November.

Her own words open the proposal. She sees at once that you listened.

The price is measured against her numbers, not your hours. A sale month with one order in ten lost costs her about ₹3.6 lakh. ₹48,000 looks small next to that.

Send it with a short email and a walk-through

The email is short, because the proposal does the talking.

Subject: Indigo Thread: 3 options for faster mobile pages (valid till 25 November)

Hi Asha, thanks for the call. The attached proposal has three options: ₹30,000, ₹48,000 and ₹75,000. I recommend the middle one, because it covers the whole store before your sale.

Can we take 10 minutes on Thursday at 11am to go through it? Or reply "Recommended" and I will send the agreement and deposit invoice today.

On the walk-through, share your screen and go through the proposal top to bottom. Then stop and ask: "Which option feels right to you?"

A 10-minute walk-through wins more often than a PDF read alone. She can ask questions, and you can answer them before doubts grow.

Follow up on fixed days

Most proposals do not get a quick answer. Founders are busy, and silence rarely means no. So decide the days in advance and write them in your lead tracker.

  • Day 0 (Wednesday): send the proposal.
  • Day 1: the walk-through. If she missed it, offer two new times.
  • Day 5: one useful detail.
  • Day 9: a reminder of the sale date.
  • Day 14: the closing note, on the day the proposal ends.

Day 5:

Hi Asha, one thing I noticed: your product page loads a reviews app twice. Removing the second copy is part of every option. Happy to answer any questions.

Day 9:

Hi Asha, a quick note on timing. To finish safely before the 5 December sale, I would need to start by 16 November. Would you like me to hold those dates?

Each message adds something new. Never send "just checking in".

When she says "I need to think"

"I need to think about it" usually means one of three things. The price feels high, someone else must agree, or a doubt was not answered.

Ask which one, kindly and directly:

Of course. So I can help, is it mainly the price, the timing, or something you need to check with someone?

If it is the price, offer a smaller option, not a discount. Essential at ₹30,000 still fixes the worst pages. More on this is on The client wants it cheaper, or free.

If someone else must agree, give her one paragraph to forward:

Slow mobile pages before the sale. Recommended option, ₹48,000, done by 27 November, measured before and after, 50% to start.

Happy to join a 10-minute call with both of you.

When to stop

Stop after the day 14 note. If she says "no" at any point, stop at once.

Hi Asha, I have not heard back, so I will assume the timing is not right and close this on my side. No hard feelings. If it comes back, just reply and I will refresh the options.

This closing note often gets a reply, because people do not like loose ends.

If she says no, thank her and ask one question: "Was it mainly price, timing or fit?" Her answer helps your next proposal.

Then set a reminder for 60 to 90 days later. Send something useful then, like a short speed checklist before the next sale.

Many "no" answers become "yes" a few months later. The messages are in the lost-deal template.
