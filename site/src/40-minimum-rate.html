Your minimum rate is the lowest hourly price that still pays for your life. This page works it out with real numbers. At the end you will have three rates to keep in mind: floor, normal and target.

The sum, one step at a time

You want ₹1 lakh a month to live on and save. You are working alone from Pune, with no employer paying for anything.

Money to live on:     ₹1,00,000 × 12 months      = ₹12,00,000 a year
Work costs (yearly):
  laptop, spread over 3 years                      ₹40,000
  software and AI tools                            ₹40,000
  internet and phone                               ₹15,000
  health and accident insurance                    ₹25,000
  chartered accountant                             ₹15,000
  domain, email, small items                       ₹15,000
  total work costs                               = ₹1,50,000
Income tax at this level                         = ₹0
Money you must receive: ₹12,00,000 + ₹1,50,000   = ₹13,50,000 a year

Weeks you work:   52 − 6 (holidays, illness)     = 46 weeks
Hours a week                                      = 40 hours
All your hours:   46 × 40                        = 1,840 hours
Share a client pays for in year one               = 55%
Paid hours:       1,840 × 0.55                   = 1,012 hours

Minimum rate:     ₹13,50,000 ÷ 1,012             = ₹1,334 an hour

So in your first year, ₹1,334 an hour is the line. Below it, you are paying to work.

A day of 8 paid hours at this rate is about ₹10,700. Rounding up to ₹1,350 an hour makes it easy to remember.

Each line above is a choice you can change. The next sections explain the two that surprise most new freelancers: the tax line and the paid share of hours.

Why the tax line is zero

India has a simple tax scheme for professionals like you. It treats half of what you receive as your profit, and you do not list expenses.

Money received                  ₹13,50,000
Half counted as profit:  ÷ 2  = ₹6,75,000
Tax-free line (with rebate)     ₹12,00,000
₹6,75,000 is below ₹12,00,000 → income tax ₹0

This holds until you receive about ₹24 lakh in a year. The full steps are on Income tax, step by step.

Why only about half your hours are paid

At your old job, every hour was paid. Meetings, quiet weeks and training all counted.

Now a client pays only for hours spent on their work. Everything else is unpaid but still needed.

  • Finding jobs and writing proposals: about 2 hours a day in Stage 1.
  • Calls, messages and small changes that nobody bills.
  • Invoices, tax and the monthly money check.
  • Learning, and gaps between jobs.

Paid time of 50–60% is normal in year one. That is why salary ÷ 160 hours is the wrong sum for a freelancer.

The share grows as you get repeat clients and spend less time hunting. Here is the same sum in a later year, when 65% of your hours are paid.

Paid hours:     1,840 × 0.65          = 1,196 hours
Minimum rate:   ₹13,50,000 ÷ 1,196    = ₹1,129 an hour

Same life, same costs, a lower minimum. Redo this sum every April, or when your costs or your paid share change.

The same sum in dollars, and Upwork's fee

Most of your first work will be on Upwork, paid in dollars. Use ₹95 to $1 here, and check the live rate.

Minimum rate in rupees              ₹1,334 an hour
In dollars:  ₹1,334 ÷ 95          = $14.04 an hour

Upwork keeps a fee on each contract. It is between 0% and 15%, shown on the job before you apply, as of November 2026. Because you are in India, Upwork also adds 18% service tax (GST) on that fee.

A job with a 10% fee, at your $40 profile rate:

Client pays for 1 hour                         $40.00
Upwork fee:  10% of $40                      − $4.00
GST on the fee:  18% of $4.00                − $0.72
You keep                                     = $35.28
In rupees:  $35.28 × 95                      = ₹3,352 an hour

The quick rule: with a 10% fee, you keep 88.2% of the price. That is 100% − 10% − 1.8%.

To keep your $14.04 minimum:  $14.04 ÷ 0.882  = $15.92 an hour

So your costs are not the problem on Upwork. The market is. Clients there pay $35 and up for good React work, and Upwork's own page shows a React median of $63 an hour.

That is why your Upwork floor is $35, not $16. Even your first small jobs pay more than twice your minimum. Use the extra hours to build proof and earn reviews.

Your three numbers

Write these on a note near your desk. Quote from the middle, never from the bottom.

NumberIndian clientsUpwork and foreign clients
Floor: never go below₹1,200 an hour$35 an hour
Normal: what you quote₹1,500–2,000 an hour$40–50 an hour
Target: after 5+ good reviews₹2,500 an hour$55–70 an hour

Your worked minimum, ₹1,334, sits just above the ₹1,200 floor. Take work between the two only when it is short and brings a review or a case study.

If a client wants less, cut the work, not the rate. The page The client wants it cheaper, or free shows how.

Your own numbers will differ. Put them into the rate calculator: money to live on, work costs and paid share of hours.

Do this now

  • List your real yearly work costs. Change the amounts above where yours differ.
  • Enter your numbers in the rate calculator and note your minimum.
  • Write your floor, normal and target rates on one note.
  • Before each Upwork bid, check the fee on the job and work out what you keep.
