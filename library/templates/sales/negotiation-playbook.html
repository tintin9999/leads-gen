Negotiation playbook

Open the .md file

Principle: price is the last thing that moves. Trade scope, time, terms or proof first. Every concession gets something back, in writing.

Before the conversation (5 minutes)

Write downExample
Target price (your Recommended option)₹48,000
Walk-away price (floor = ₹1,200/hr × honest hours × 1.2 buffer; global floor $35/hr)₹32,000
Your alternative if they say no (your pipeline)3 other proposals out, 2 calls this week
What you can trade (see the give/get table)Remove app cleanup; 100% upfront; testimonial
What you won't tradeDeposit, IP-on-full-payment, written scope

A full pipeline is your negotiation strength. If you're desperate, the client will feel it. Keep prospecting even while you negotiate.

Scripts

"Can you do it for less?"

"I can't lower the price for the same scope, but I can make it fit. If we drop {{cart/checkout work and the 60-day care}}, the Essential option is ₹30,000 and still gets your product pages fast before the sale. Would that work?"

"Your competitor quoted half."

"Then they're quoting a different scope, or they know something I don't. Could you share what's included? If it's truly the same, go with them, and I mean that."

"That's too high." (no number)

"What did you have in mind?" (Then stay quiet. Don't negotiate against yourself.)

A number far below your floor

"At ₹{{x}} I'd be doing a rushed job, and that wouldn't be fair to you. What I can do for that is a written audit with the top 5 fixes your team can apply."

Calibrated questions (Chris Voss), when stuck:

  • "How am I supposed to do that for half the budget?"
  • "What would need to be true for the Recommended option to work for you?"
  • "What's the biggest risk you see in going ahead?"

Labelling (name the emotion, then pause):

  • "It sounds like you've been burned by a developer before."
  • "It seems like timing is the real pressure here, not the price."

India-specific notes

  • Expect a counter from D2C founders. It's culture, not an insult. Don't pad your prices. Sell fixed packages and answer the counter with scope.
  • "Last price?" → "This is my fixed price for this scope. Here's the leaner option if budget is the constraint."
  • Mention GST/TDS early so the final number doesn't surprise anyone: "₹48,000 + GST if applicable. If you deduct TDS, that's fine; just share Form 16A."
  • WhatsApp negotiation is normal, but confirm the final terms by email the same day.

Global-specific notes

  • Price in USD (or GBP/EUR). Never quote INR to a foreign client.
  • Transfer fees: "Please send with OUR charges", or build ~2% into the price.
  • Bigger firms: procurement may ask for a 10% "standard discount". Price that in up front, or trade it for Net 7 → Net 30 only if cash flow allows.

After you agree

  • Recap the final scope, price, dates and terms by email within the hour.
  • Update the proposal/SOW so the document matches what was said.
  • Send the agreement + deposit invoice together.
  • CRM: value = agreed price; note what you traded and why.

Never

  • Never give a discount without getting something back.
  • Never start work before the deposit "to show goodwill".
  • Never accept "we'll sort out the price later".
  • Never let a negotiation run past 2 rounds by text. Get on a 10-minute call.
